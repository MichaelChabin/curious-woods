After the Ice

About twelve thousand years ago, the ice that covered the north began to melt, and it did not come back in the winter. This page counts years from then. Everything people have made since then fits on the line at the top: farms, cities, writing, ships, all of it.

The lines are the whole of the Earth’s time, in periods, each period a band in its own colour. Tap a period and it opens as a line beneath; tap it again and that line goes. Drag the window’s ends to take in more or less: the line beneath says how long that is. Tap a line’s title to fold it to a strip. The last line is the twelve thousand years since the ice let go, spread out below its window, with the copper handle for the year you are in. The map shows the world in that year: this side of the ice ages the ground, past them the earth from space, with a word under it saying how sure that is. Tap a mark to read what happened there, and More for the story.
