Twelve
Twelve notes on two tiers, all one colour, six keys to an octave. Put home wherever you like; the shape of a scale is rigid and slides with it.
First instrument of the Sound Series. Touch version — chords with several fingers, glissando by dragging.
added 2026-08-08
Twelve — MIDI
The same lattice, lit by a MIDI keyboard, four octaves wide, with frequencies on the keys. Play a note and the one six columns along: the number doubles while the ear says same note, higher.
Chrome only — Safari has no Web MIDI. On a file:// URL it may need serving from localhost.
added 2026-08-08
Canvas Panes
A load test: how much of a frame does it cost to draw a table of leaded panes while panning and zooming? Reports draw time against the 16.7ms budget, because frame rate alone reads 60fps until things are already bad.
Bench for the plane extraction — the one that could have blocked moving Glass Multiplication to canvas. It didn't.
Fills and Light
Selection lives in the lead; discovery dims everything else. Hunts the opacity at which a dimmed pane reads as quiet rather than broken, and compares three ways of dimming against the current glow.
Bench for the plane extraction — the answer is a number to be carried into the design.
Prime Glass
Prime colours across four workshops, stripes ordered smallest prime to largest, with a monochrome toggle. Asks whether arrangement alone can tell 6, 10 and 14 apart when colour is switched off.
Bench for the plane extraction — if colour turns out to carry delight rather than information, that is a much stronger position than a special palette.
Prime Tones — 11 and 13
A listening bench for the primes Glass Multiplication does not yet have: four candidate ways to sound 11 and 13, heard against the four that exist.
Open question for v2.2 — an octave is perceptually the same note, so the standing proposal risks 11 sounding like 2. Nothing here is wired into the lab.
Number Theory Lab
Multiplication and division as rectangles, on a number plane you can pan across.
Its grid and axis rendering is the visual reference for Glass Multiplication v2.2.
Beads Around a Circle
How many beads fit around a circle — count them, divide circumference by diameter, and keep the past measurements.
Clinks
Someone arrives, someone leaves, and everyone clinks glasses: the triangular numbers, counted out.
