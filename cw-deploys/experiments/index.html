Six Against Five
Two click tracks at a fixed whole-number ratio and one speed knob. At two a second it is a rhythm you can count; at two hundred and fifty it is a chord. Both voices use the identical click — only the rate differs — so nothing changes across the sweep but speed. Brightness runs the tone from kazoo to reed; seven ratios run from the octave down to 45:32, which never lines up at all.
Bench for the Sound Series (Sound-Rhythm-Roll-Aug21): can the ratios be delivered by counting rather than by waves? No sine, no spectrum, no pressure — the wave material is deferred to timbre, where it is actually wanted. The reading below the bench is the first sample of the child voice for this material. Open: no tapping, and no way yet to hear a true ratio against the keyboard’s approximation of it.

Map Reveal
The coordinate grid constructed in front of you — two seed points, full circles rolling down the axes, every line earning its crossing circles, accelerating as the numbers grow. Knobs for duration, the legible opening, and acceleration; zoom in far enough and the tenths construct themselves.
Bench for the map reveal (Decisions-Controls-Aug12, §8, §11, §15). It settles the tempo by looking. Every step is in the log; speed compresses time, never content.
added 2026-08-13
The Fog Map
Every pane shaded by the rank of its largest prime factor — white at 2, deepening toward black at the 256th. Drag and scroll out as far as you like: the map refuses to go dark, because almost everything is built from small primes.
A magnitude viewing rather than a factor-reading one. It asks how big, not which — and the stripes running the full width are the prime gaps.
Canvas Panes
A load test: how much of a frame does it cost to draw a table of leaded panes while panning and zooming? Reports draw time against the 16.7ms budget, because frame rate alone reads 60fps until things are already bad.
Bench for the plane extraction — the one that could have blocked moving Glass Multiplication to canvas. It didn't.
Fills and Light
Two declared palettes, a resting one and a lit one, both derived from the workshop's own colour so hue never moves. Showing a set — or selecting a pane — lights its members. Nothing is dimmed to make anything else stand out.
Bench for the plane extraction. Dimming was tried and abandoned: every procedural route to a quiet colour computes one nobody chose.
Prime Glass
Prime colours across four workshops, stripes ordered smallest prime to largest, with a monochrome toggle. Asks whether arrangement alone can tell 6, 10 and 14 apart when colour is switched off.
Bench for the plane extraction — if colour turns out to carry delight rather than information, that is a much stronger position than a special palette.
Prime Tones — 11 and 13
A listening bench for the primes Glass Multiplication does not yet have: four candidate ways to sound 11 and 13, heard against the four that exist.
Settled here by listening: the octave works, a child can tell C from C′. Timbre stayed unspent as a result, and is still reserved.

Multiplication and division as rectangles, on a number plane you can pan across.
Its grid and axis rendering was the behavioural reference for the shared plane — the adaptive step driving labels and gridlines from one function.
