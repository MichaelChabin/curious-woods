Van Gogh's Palette for "The Starry Night"

Saint-Rémy-de-Provence, June 1889

Blues - The Night Sky
Prussian Blue
Ferric ferrocyanide (Fe₇(CN)₁₈)
The deep, rich blues of the swirling night sky. Van Gogh's workhorse blue for dark values.
RGB: (30, 58, 95)
Cobalt Blue
Cobalt(II) aluminate (CoAl₂O₄)
Used for the lighter spiraling stars and middle tones. More expensive but brilliant.
RGB: (0, 71, 171)
French Ultramarine
Synthetic lapis lazuli (Na₈Al₆Si₆O₂₄S₄)
The warm, brilliant blue for the large star near the crescent moon.
RGB: (46, 80, 144)
Yellows - Stars and Moon
Chrome Yellow
Lead chromate (PbCrO₄)
Brilliant yellow for the moon and bright stars. Van Gogh loved this despite its toxicity.
RGB: (255, 200, 46)
Cadmium Yellow (early version)
Cadmium sulfide (CdS)
New and expensive in Van Gogh's time. Used for the glowing halos around stars.
RGB: (255, 215, 0)
Yellow Ochre
Hydrated iron oxide (Fe₂O₃·H₂O)
Earth pigment for warmer, muted yellows in the village lights.
RGB: (196, 160, 0)
Greens - Cypress and Landscape
Emerald Green
Copper acetoarsenite (Cu(C₂H₃O₂)₂·3Cu(AsO₂)₂)
Bright, transparent green for the fluid, wavy hills. Highly toxic.
RGB: (80, 200, 120)
Viridian
Hydrated chromium oxide (Cr₂O₃·2H₂O)
Dark bluish-green for shadows and the silhouette of the cypress tree.
RGB: (64, 130, 109)
Whites and Earth Tones
Lead White (Blanc d'Argent)
Basic lead carbonate (2PbCO₃·Pb(OH)₂)
Primary white for mixing and the impasto technique on stars. Toxic but unmatched in handling.
RGB: (255, 254, 247)
Zinc White
Zinc oxide (ZnO)
Cooler, more transparent white for glazing effects.
RGB: (250, 255, 245)
Ivory Black
Carbon from charred bone
Used sparingly for the darkest outlines and mixing deep tones.
RGB: (28, 28, 28)

Van Gogh's Painting Technique:

Van Gogh applied these pigments with bold, expressive brushstrokes, often straight from the tube for maximum intensity. The thick impasto technique he used for the stars made them literally stand out from the canvas, catching light at different angles.

He wrote to his brother Theo: "I have limited myself to simple colors... but I refrained from buying colours one ought to mix oneself." Yet for "The Starry Night," he used some of the most vivid and expensive pigments available, showing the importance of this work to him.

Historical Note: Many of Van Gogh's pigments were highly toxic. Lead white, chrome yellow, and emerald green contained lead, chromium, and arsenic respectively. Modern artists use safer alternatives, but these original pigments contributed to the unique luminosity and texture of Van Gogh's work.
