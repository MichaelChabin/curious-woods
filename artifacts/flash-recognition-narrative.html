Three thousand years ago, shepherds in the hills of ancient Greece had a problem.
They needed to know if all their sheep came home each evening. But counting takes time, and wolves don't wait.
So they discovered something remarkable.
They found they could look at a small group of sheep and instantly know how many there were.
Not count. Not calculate. Just... know.
Three sheep? They knew. Four? They knew. Five? Still instant.

Scientists have a name for what you're doing: subitizing.
It means "sudden knowing" - seeing a number without counting.
Your brain has special circuits, probably in the parietal cortex, that recognize patterns of up to about four or five objects instantly.
It's not math. It's older than math. It's pattern recognition so fundamental that babies can do it.
