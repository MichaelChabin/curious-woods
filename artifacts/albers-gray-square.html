The surrounding colors completely change how we perceive the gray. The warm yellows make it appear cooler and darker. The reds make it appear warmer and lighter.

Albers' Method

Josef Albers painted hundreds of these nested square compositions, which he called "Homage to the Square." Each painting was a scientific experiment in color perception.

By nesting squares within squares, he created what he called "color environments" - the gray square doesn't just sit next to other colors, it's surrounded by them, intensifying the effect.

The off-center placement (weighted toward the bottom) creates visual tension and prevents the eye from settling, making the color interactions even more dynamic.

"In order to use color effectively it is necessary to recognize that color deceives continually. To this end, the beginning is not a study of color systems, but rather, a study of the interaction of color." - Josef Albers, Interaction of Color (1963)

Why This Matters

This isn't just about art. This principle affects everything we see:

  • Why the same paint looks different on different walls
  • Why colors in logos must be tested on various backgrounds
  • Why your phone screen looks blue at night but normal during the day
  • Why artists care so much about the color of gallery walls

Albers taught that we never really see a "true" color - we only see colors in relationship to other colors. Every color experience is relative.
