# Time-passing — Naomi Devil's discs, and the moiré between them

Michael, 30 Sept 2026: he would like to see the sculpture in operation, and is wondering about
a page for it on curiouswoods.org. He has cropped the Wikimedia photograph for an icon.

**The thing.** *Idő-töltés* (*Time-passing*), Naomi Devil, 2016, a cybernetic sculpture made for
the Hungarikon collection round Nicolas Schöffer's own hourglass. The artist's description: a
motor turns laser-engraved acrylic discs, lit by LEDs, whose radial stripes make a moiré that
recalls the infinity sign; the hourglass turns with the mount so its sand flows in endless
circulation. Op art of the 1960s, and the wiring left showing, for cybernetics' own period.

**What is built.** `cw-deploys/experiments/time-passing-bench.html`: two radial gratings over a
light, one turning, with a few more spokes than the other (the difference sets how many lobes
the moiré has: two more spokes, two lobes, the figure of eight); the hourglass on a mount that
turns once every fifty seconds, its sand running into whichever bulb is lower. Words for the
spoke count, the difference, the motor's speed, one disc alone, and a drag to turn the disc by
hand. No film of the real work was found, so the counts and speeds are the page's, not the
sculpture's.

**What it opens onto.** Moiré as a measuring instrument (two gratings tell a movement smaller
than either can show); why the pattern turns faster than the disc, which is beats in space as a
tuning fork's are beats in time (Sound Series); Schöffer himself, the first cybernetic sculpture
(CYSP 1, 1956), and the Kalocsa collection; an hourglass that never finishes as a picture of
time on the Time Machine.

**Rights.** The photograph is Darabos György's, uploaded by the artist to Wikimedia Commons under
CC BY-SA 4.0: usable with credit, and any crop of it (the icon) carries the same licence and the
credit. The sculpture is the artist's own copyright; a simulation of its mechanism is not a copy
of it, but a page about it should name her and link the photograph, and a story would want her
yes for more than that.

**Undecided.** Whether this is a bench, a story (*Time-passing*, with Schöffer and the Hungarikon
collection as its world), or a piece of the Perception Lab. Whether `lab: perception` is right for
an optical bench, or whether maps-and-time claims it for the hourglass.
