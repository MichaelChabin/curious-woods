# The Mirror and the Map

Draft exists: `_msc/_mscVault/1 CW/Story/The Mirror & the Map.md`. Complete, in Michael's
voice, second person throughout.

A grandmother gives you a small square mirror a week before she dies — bronze frame, worn
symbols, scratches on the back that might be a circle and some crescents. Handed down
grandmother to grandmother "back to the time of giants and ice and magic." She says: *the
next time you see me, you'll know its secret.* You never see her again. The mirror becomes
clutter you feel guilty about.

Then the flu, three days in bed, and at 2 a.m. under a full moon the mirror is on your
pillow with your grandmother in it, smiling, pointing at the window behind her. You hold
it up to the moonlight and see a glacier calving into a boulder-strewn lake, and mammoths
grazing on a gravel hill close enough to hear the grass tear.

The last lines: **the mirror is your imagination, the map is the coordinate plane, and the
magic happens when you use them together.**

## Why this was shelved, and why that was wrong

Michael set it aside when he stopped introducing the multiplication *table*. But the story
does not end on the table. It ends on **the coordinate plane** — which is now, per
`1. Redesign.md`, the thesis of the entire system. The labs are viewings of one plane. The
story was written for the architecture before the architecture was settled.

Its central move is also exactly the argument the plane has to win: *an unremarkable
lattice that everyone walks past is the most powerful tool ever invented, and the magic is
in knowing how to use it.* That is the plane's pitch, delivered without a single word of
instruction.

## The accident worth noticing

"The time of giants and ice and magic" is roughly 10,000 BCE, which is **Year 0** in
`CW-Date-Convention.md`. The mirror looks back to precisely the origin of the project's
own timeline. Nobody planned that. It makes the story a natural entry to T1, the anchor
years, from the far end.

## Tone note

It does not announce, quiz, or congratulate. It withholds the meaning until the last two
lines and then delivers it flat, without explanation. Worth keeping beside Biomorphs as a
reference for how child-facing writing should sound.

## Open

- Where does it sit? Opening the plane, rather than opening multiplication.
- The reveal is currently two bare lines after a gap. Does it want more air, or is the
  bluntness the point? Probably the bluntness.
- Does the mirror recur — a device the child can pick up again — or is it spent in one
  telling? A recurring mirror risks becoming a mascot. Spent is safer.
