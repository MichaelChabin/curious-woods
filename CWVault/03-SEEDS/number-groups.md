# Number groups — the census, and the representation finding

Captured 18 Aug 2026 from Michael's weekend lists (Denver, iPad) and the session
that followed. This is the input the Multiplication walk is waiting on: the
groups the Properties/lens panels will feature, and the finding that shapes how
they light.

## The finding

**A group is not a set of numbers — it is a set of numbers plus a chosen way of
standing.** Displaying every way to make 16 was exactly what was asked for, and
it makes some properties harder to see: sometimes 16 wants to be 2×8, sometimes
only 4².

On the plane this is not a display option but a geometric fact: a number's
representations are *places*. 16 lives at (2,8), (4,4), (8,2), (1,16), (16,1).
A lens therefore declares two things: **which numbers, and which of their homes
light.**

## Three species of group

**Geometric — the plane draws them itself.** The home choice is built in.

- Multiples of n — entire rows and columns. (Evens come free as multiples of 2.)
- Powers of n — the doubling/tripling ladders.
- n(n+k) — **the constant-difference diagonals as one kind of thing**: squares
  are k=0 (the main diagonal), pronics k=1, and so on outward. Compresses what
  were separate curiosities into one family. (The fog seed's spine-and-siblings.)
- Triangular numbers — the halves of pronics; a pronic rectangle cut
  corner-to-corner, one gesture from Restack's world.
- Constant-sum diagonals (top-left to bottom-right): x rises as y falls, sum
  fixed. Products are not arithmetic but their gaps are the consecutive odds
  (sum 10: 9, 16, 21, 24, 25 — gaps 7, 5, 3, 1); the square in the middle is
  the largest product (every fence problem, discovered); and every product is
  the middle square minus a small square — 24 = 25−1, 21 = 25−4. Multiplication
  as difference of squares; how the Babylonians multiplied and Fermat factored.
  **No control implication — one lens among lenses.** *Confirmed by Michael,
  18 Aug: featured, not speculative.*

**Shadow — defined by absence.** May want a different visual voice; open below.

- Odd numbers — the complement of the multiples of 2; they live only where both
  coordinates are odd.
- Primes — a number whose curve of homes misses the lattice everywhere except
  1×p and p×1. (What the fog map was showing.)

**List — sequences imported onto the plane.** No geometric home.

- Fibonacci.
- 3x+1 trajectories, maybe.
- Divisor count / highly composite numbers (12, 60, 360) — the strongest
  addition, because it is native to the plane: **a number's divisor count is how
  many homes it has.** Count the lit panes. Primes have two; 60 has twelve, and
  there is why the Babylonians chose it. (Sits between species: the set is
  defined by counting homes, but membership is a list.)

## Open questions

1. **The home rule for list groups.** When 8 lights because Fibonacci says so,
   which home lights — all of them, the axis, the squarest? A default is needed
   and it is a real decision.
2. **Do shadow groups get their own visual voice?** They light by what isn't
   there; the fog map is the existing answer for one of them.
3. **Which groups ship first** in the Properties panels, and what each declares
   its colour to mean (per-viewing declaration, Fog ledger).
4. Groups that imply *modified processes* — Michael's anxiety that some groups
   change how controls are built. So far none has: everything above is a lens
   (numbers + home rule). Watch for the first counterexample.
