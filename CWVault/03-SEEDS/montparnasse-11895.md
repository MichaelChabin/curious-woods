# Montparnasse — the train in the street

22 October 1895. The Granville express runs late, the driver brakes late, the air brake
does not hold. The engine crosses the concourse, breaks the façade, and falls thirty feet
into the Place de Rennes, where it stands on its nose while the carriages remain politely
inside the station. One person is killed, on the street, by falling masonry — not a
passenger.

The photograph is the most famous railway image ever made, which is the first hook: a
child has almost certainly seen it without knowing it was real.

## What it opens onto

**Momentum, and why the crash is a physics problem rather than a story about a careless
driver.** Mass times velocity, the energy that has to go somewhere, and the fact that the
carriages stopping and the engine not stopping is the interesting part.

**Ground as a medium.** Michael's note: the impact was felt across Paris. Whether that is
literally seismic or merely structural, the question — *how far does a thump travel, and
through what* — is a good one, and it connects to sound as a wave in a solid, which
connects to the Perception Lab's pitch work by a route nobody would guess from either end.

**Why it was photographed at all.** Cameras were where they were in 1895 for reasons, and
those reasons are the same reasons the Lumières were about to project a train at an
audience two months later. Process over product: someone had to carry the plate camera
down there.
