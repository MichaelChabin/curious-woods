## Round 1: the sort

The rule, before each bin: does the picture answer a question the More raises, and is it the real thing the More stands on. One picture per More; two where the More raises two questions.

**1. Molten** (`period-molten`). The More opens on the magma ocean ("there was nowhere to stand") and says almost nothing survives but zircons.
- A painting of the magma ocean with the close Moon: **findable, a drawing.** NASA has public-domain paintings of the early Earth; the insert names a magma ocean as a reconstruction it allows, captioned as a drawing and whose. No photograph can answer "what did it look like", and she cannot picture it. Question 1.
- A Jack Hills zircon: **findable, to try.** Commons holds "Jack Hills detrital zircons BSE micrographs" (from a paper; licence to read). Valley's cathodoluminescence picture of the 4.4-billion-year crystal is *ask*. The More names no site, so a Jack Hills grain is the real kind of thing it stands on ("tiny crystals of zircon"); the caption says which grains and how old the paper says they are.
- Proposed: the zircon; the painting if Michael says yes.

**2. Oceans** (`period-oceans`). The More opens at Strelley Pool on the mounds.
- The Strelley Pool stromatolites: **findable.** Commons holds several specimens labelled Strelley Pool Formation, 3.35 to 3.46 billion years (museum photographs; licence and museum to read); an outcrop photograph at the pool is better and is tried first.
- Living stromatolites at Hamelin Pool: **none, proposed.** A lookalike, and the More makes no comparison with living mounds; the insert allows one only when it does. Question 2.
- Proposed: Strelley Pool.
