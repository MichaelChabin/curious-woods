## Circles

### Fill by face   · 3 Oct 2026 · candidate
**Idea.** Instead of selecting edges to lay lead, tap any open space and it fills to whatever edges enclose it. A new shape drawn across a coloured face splits it; both halves keep the colour until retapped.
**Where it sits.** Circles level, Glass Geometry; applies to every level after.
**Opens.** One tap colours a pane of any size, including the tiny ones that made lead selection tedious. Same gesture for circles, lines, grid, rectangles, regions.
**Costs.** The lab must compute faces of the full arrangement (the bench already cuts panes at crossings in exact fractions). Decide what tapping the open plane does.
**Source.** Michael, chat of 3 Oct, from fresh eyes on the app.

### Lead is where colours meet   · 3 Oct 2026 · loose
**Idea.** Lead is not a thing she selects. It appears wherever two colours meet; two faces the same colour have only a construction line between them. "Delete an edge" becomes "colour both sides the same".
**Where it sits.** Circles level, Glass; with fill by face.
**Opens.** Removes the keep/lose gesture entirely. Deleting is never needed, so construction points are never lost.
**Costs.** Real glaziers do lead between panes of the same colour; there may need to be a way to ask for it. May be too clever for a child; untested.
**Source.** Claude, chat of 3 Oct, in response to fill by face.

### Readout only while the mouse is down   · 3 Oct 2026 · candidate
**Idea.** A circle shows its radius and area only while she is dragging. Nothing stays on screen to aim at.
**Where it sits.** Circles level now; the same rule for rectangles and triangles at the rectangles level.
**Opens.** Answers the aiming worry in Worknote-Glass-Grid-24Sept item 13. Radius 1, area 3.14; radius 2, area 12.57 — pi arrives by her dividing, not by being told.
**Costs.** Nothing known.
**Source.** Michael, experiment of 3 Oct, built that morning.

## Lines

### The four-point engine   · 3 Oct 2026 · candidate
**Idea.** Three points make three lines and stop. Four points make six lines, three new crossings, and from there lines alone grow a net as fine as you like, everywhere. A story about watching that happen.
**Where it sits.** Lines level, after Wright's Light Screens (which opens lines). Second story on the level.
**Opens.** Shows lines' strength and their hole together: the net reaches everywhere but cannot find a middle, which is why the circle is needed. Sets up level `both`.
**Costs.** Needs a decision on how many points the lines level opens with (three lets her feel the stall; four, as in Wright's frame, means the engine was there all along).
**Source.** Michael, chat of 3 Oct; counting corrected (six lines, not sixteen).

## Between levels

### Dragging a point   · 3 Oct 2026 · candidate
**Idea.** Points can be dragged, and everything built from them follows. For four points the whole net moves and stays the same net; a midpoint does not survive the drag.
**Where it sits.** A new capability, its own door, between lines and both. Michael: two stories, lines first, dragging second.
**Opens.** Later levels get it for free: drag a rectangle's corner, a triangle's vertex. Bridge to the invariance idea.
**Costs.** A new gesture, so by the one rule it needs its own story. Engineering is replaying the construction log from the moved point forward; not free.
**Source.** Michael, chat of 3 Oct, ruled two stories.

## Other labs

### Make your own palette   · 4 Oct 2026 · candidate
**Idea.** Every tool offers a default palette and *New*. New shows a row of pictures; she picks one, taps the colours she wants, and keeps the result as a palette beside the default. As many as she likes. Her own pictures are an option. Leaning: five slots with a "+" for more (working choice, not ruled).
**Where it sits.** Colour lab capability, borrowed by every lab where colour is her choice (Glass windows, rectangles, Cézanne). Same gesture as the colour sampler in the painting stories, plus a *keep*.
**Opens.** Her palettes go everywhere she makes something; a palette remembers where each colour was picked, so it leads back to its painting and story. A possible door to the Colour lab.
**Costs.** One shelf of palettes that belongs to her, not to a lab, so it must be stored. Not for places where colour carries meaning (maps, plots, the three lamps) — consistent with the 18 Sept colour ruling. Her own pictures stay on her device; only the colours travel when she shares (copyright and privacy). Average a small patch, not one pixel, or the picks come out muddy. Existing `palettes.json` palettes run six to eight colours, so the count needs settling.
**Source.** Michael, chat of 4 Oct.

## Across the whole

### One gesture per story   · 3 Oct 2026 · candidate
**Idea.** Each story in the geometry series introduces exactly one new gesture, so partial versions of the lab can appear in stories as the way the whole thing is learned.
**Where it sits.** Across the geometry spine; a sharpening of the one-door rule.
**Opens.** The question "has she met this yet?" always has an answer at the level of hands, not just capabilities.
**Costs.** Some capabilities are the same gesture with a different result (lines where circles were); some are new gestures (drag). Keep the distinction.
**Source.** Michael, chat of 3 Oct.

### A job that needs the attention   · 4 Oct 2026 · candidate
**Idea.** Give her a task with its own reason to exist whose doing requires the close attention, and never ask for the attention. Picking a palette makes her look at a painting closely without being asked to; sorting a limerick's lines is easy but makes her think about them.
**Where it sits.** Across the whole; a test for any proposed tool or story.
**Opens.** A question to put to every new bench: what does doing it make her notice?
**Costs.** Ruined by a follow-up question ("what did you notice?"). The made thing (the palette, the finished poem) is the only follow-up.
**Source.** Michael, chat of 4 Oct, comparing the palette maker to the limerick puzzle.
