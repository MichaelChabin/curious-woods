## Circles

### Fill by face   · 3 Oct 2026 · candidate
**Idea.** Instead of selecting edges to lay lead, tap any open space and it fills to whatever edges enclose it. A new shape drawn across a coloured face splits it; both halves keep the colour until retapped.
**Where it sits.** Circles level, Glass Geometry; applies to every level after.
**Opens.** One tap colours a pane of any size, including the tiny ones that made lead selection tedious. Same gesture for circles, lines, grid, rectangles, regions.
**Costs.** The lab must compute faces of the full arrangement (the bench already cuts panes at crossings in exact fractions). Decide what tapping the open plane does.
**Source.** Michael, chat of 3 Oct, from fresh eyes on the app.

### Lead is where colours meet   · 3 Oct 2026 · loose
**Idea.** Lead is not a thing she selects. It appears wherever two colours meet; two faces the same colour have only a construction line between them. "Delete an edge" becomes "colour both sides the same".

**Costs.** Real glaziers do lead between panes of the same colour; there may need to be a way to ask for it. May be too clever for a child; untested.
**Source.** Claude, chat of 3 Oct, in response to fill by face.

**Idea.** Three points make three lines and stop. Four points make six lines, three new crossings, and from there lines alone grow a net as fine as you like, everywhere. A story about watching that happen.
**Where it sits.** Lines level, after Wright's Light Screens (which opens lines). Second story on the level.

### Drag for lines, not for circles, never once there is glass   · 4 Oct 2026 · ruled
**Idea.** Points can be dragged, and everything built from them follows, but only at the lines level and only while the construction is uncoloured. Circles are excluded. Once the first pane is filled, nothing moves.
**Where it sits.** Lines level, as its own door (the second lines story). Returns at the rectangles level, where corners are free points and dragging is cheap.
**Opens.** The four-point net: drag any point and the whole net moves and stays the same net; the midpoint does not survive, which is why the circle is needed next. A natural order of work: drag until it looks right, then colour.
**Costs.** Two reasons dragging stops where it does. Two circles meet in two places, and under a drag the chosen crossing slides, swaps or vanishes; lines never do that. Moving edges merges and splits faces, and nobody can say which half keeps the yellow. The glazier gives the rule: once the glass is in, the lead is set.
**Source.** Michael and Claude, chat of 4 Oct. Michael: it would not have been found until tried and failed. *(Merged from the holding pen, 6 Oct.)*

### Fractions as triangles   · 14 Oct 2026 · candidate

**Where it sits.** The plane; a door shared with the trig trail.
**Opens.** Fractions with Euclid's tools and no arithmetic, and equivalent fractions as similar triangles.
**Costs.** None known.

**Where it sits.** Across the whole; `claude/Standard-Interactives.md` (draft, 26 Oct). The four other 3D chats build to it.
**Opens.** Art made once at full size; no story held back by "a 5 year old platform" (Michael).
**Costs.** Each interactive needs its tier numbers and its floor named in its spec, and a test on a lower machine than Michael's.
**Source.** Michael, 25–26 Oct; the tier scheme Claude's.

### Told aloud: the element entries in a storyteller's voice (working name)   · 23 Nov 2026 · ruled
**Idea.** Michael asked what the element entries would sound like if Kipling had written them, caring more for delight than word limits. Carbon was rewritten that way; he then took out the overt Kipling and kept the delight. What survived: things a hand can touch (soot, chimneys, a pencil), a list that climbs ("A few had it as diamonds."), a turn on its own line, the strangest fact first and again last ("both of them are a quarter of you"), and saying what a person did, not what they had ("used a large lens to focus sunlight onto small diamonds, and the diamonds burned").
**Where it sits.** Across the whole: Prose-Standard.md and Prompt-Elements-Entries.md; first the ten entries of Elements batch 01, then any reference prose.
**Opens.** A reference entry she reads for pleasure and repeats to someone. A register between the plain entry and a full story.
**Costs.** Runs about half again as long as the 150 to 250 words the schema allows. Bends the standard's rule against sentences that admire themselves, so the standard needs a line saying what is wanted, or editors will flatten it. Each added detail (the carbon rising to the outer layers, Tennant weighing the gas) must be checked before it ships. Carbon's wording is Michael's by ruling.
**Source.** Michael, 23 Nov, in the editor pass 11 session; the Kipling draft was Claude's, the trimmed register and the Lavoisier sentence Michael's.
*Ruled the same evening:* Ruling-Voice-01.md. The test is whether it is read; the voice is never finished.
