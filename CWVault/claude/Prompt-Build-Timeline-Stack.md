## 3. The stack

**Words.** A line in the stack is *open* (its strip with event labels) or *closed* (its strip with short, faint event lines and no labels). Lines can also be *removed* (taken out of the stack). Keep the three words distinct in the code and on the board.

**The opening scene.** Every line down to After the ice, closed, as thin strips, with no cones. The Time Machine's Main stays the last rung, as lane C built it.

**Tapping a period** that has a line of its own opens that line beneath, named after the period, open, with a cone from the period to the new line. Any lines that were beneath are removed first.

**Tapping the same period again** removes its line and everything beneath it. It works like undo. The Year handle stays where it is.

**Stretching or shrinking the window** opens the next line automatically. If the window matches a period, the line takes the period's name; if not, it has no name, only its span in years.

**Doing anything on a line** (tapping a period, an event, or dragging the window) opens it if it is closed, and its cone appears.

**Tapping a line's title** opens or closes that line without changing the stack. Any combination is allowed; two lines far apart may both be open, with closed strips between them.

**Cones.** A cone appears between a line and the one beneath it once she has acted there, and stays while both lines remain. **Open for Michael:** after her first tap, junctions she hasn't touched have no cone. Show him that, and the alternative (her first tap brings in every cone along the path), and he rules.

This replaces lane D's *Collapse*, *Show*, *Show all* and *Collapse by itself*. Remove those words.

## 5. The map

**Names.** Remove every place name from the map at rest. Coastlines and seas stay unlabelled too.

- When she taps an event with a place, the map goes to it and shows its name. On the flat map it centres and zooms in; on the globe it turns to face her first, then zooms.
- Then the other places with events inside the selected period appear with their names, if they fall within the view. Tapping one selects its event: it lights on the timeline and its summary shows.
- A name, once shown, stays until she selects another period.

**The year.** The map always shows the world at the Year handle's year (the copper handle). The year changes when:

- she opens a period: it becomes the period's beginning;
- she drags the Year handle;
- she taps an event: it becomes the event's year;
- a graph plays (when that exists): the handle moves as it plays.

Where the map has no picture for a year, it shows today's ground and says nothing, as already ruled.

## 6. Graphs

A word *Graphs* in the column, beside *Map*. Under it, italic commands appear for the plots that can be drawn for the selected period. The plot words already loose in the column (*People*, *Sea level*, *Oxygen*, *Day length*, *Minerals*, *Sun and inside*) move under it.

- **Each curve file says where it is meaningful:** a range of years (`from`, `to`) and the fewest points it needs inside a period to draw (`minPoints`). A command appears only when the selected period holds enough of the curve, and disappears when she moves where it would be empty.
- **Tap a command to show its plot** beneath the timelines, sharing their margin (its name and unit in the margin, as a line's title) and the selected period's span. Tap again to hide it.
- **The plot follows the selection.** A new period redraws it; if the period is outside the curve's range, the plot goes, and so does its word.
- **The Year handle's line runs down through the plot.** The only number is the value at the handle, rounded, with *about*.
- **Day length** follows the selection like every other plot.
- **People** begins with *Homo sapiens*, 315,000 years ago; before that its command does not appear. Published counts begin about 12,000 years ago; before that there are only rough guesses. Draw the guesses as guesses, using the curve's `howSure`, never with the confidence of the counts.

This replaces lane E's panel. Keep from lane E and the spec's *Plots* what doesn't conflict: the band where an estimate is shaky, the faint dashed line at today's value.

## Amendments, 10 Oct (Michael's answers to Claude Code's reading; these win where they differ from the parts above)

- **Lines above the parent close by themselves.** When a line opens beneath, every line above its parent closes, so two whole lines always show; a tap on a closed strip's title reopens it, which covers the rare wish to see two lines far apart together. Without this rule drilling down reopens the seven-line stack lane D set out to cure.
- **"Main" is the After the ice line.** The word was the Time Machine's old name for its own top line, the twelve thousand years with the Focus window and the close-up (Detail) beneath. In the one timeline the Earth line is the top; the After the ice line is simply the last rung, drawn by the page because it carries the window and the close-up. It goes with the stack: when a tap above removes the lines beneath, it goes too, and comes back when After the ice is reached again (lane C). The arrival run sweeps once, at the opening scene, since After the ice is open from the first moment. Never call it Main.
- **The period words live in a store of their own,** `stories/deep-time-periods.json`, generated by `tools/periods-from-vault.py` from `Periods-Deep-01.md` and `-02.md` (and the batches to come), keyed by the period's name, the way the events are made. A period with no words yet shows its name and span and says nothing. The texts are used as they land; their checking is Michael's follow-up.
- **The globe turns to face her, and does not zoom.** Zoom on the globe is engine work for later.
- **People begins where its data begins,** 12,000 years ago; its command appears only on lines that hold that. The guessed points back to 315,000 years are a curve pass of their own, later.
- **The smear goes.** Between a billion and two hundred million years the globe drew seven copies of each piece shifted in longitude, to say longitude was uncertain; that is fog where the ruling of 7 Oct says a guess. Draw the model's own position once, keep the word *inferred*, and let the sentence under the map say the longitude is one best estimate.
- **"When were there a billion people?" comes out** of the column, with the plot machinery it rode on.
- **The clash renames** the tree asks for (*Grasslands*, *Coal*, *Forests*, *Dinosaurs*, *Flowers*) are not ruled; they wait, listed in the report.
- **At phone width** the margin title goes above the line, as lane D drew it, and the lines keep their width.
