# The Time lab bench, step 10: Michael's answers to step 9

`cw-deploys/experiments/time-lab.html` only, unless a part says otherwise. Read Report-Time-Lab-Bench-09.md first. Step 9 must be committed before you start; if it is not, stop and say so.

## Open on Complex life

The lab opens on Complex life, on every screen, not on the 4.6 billion. Michael: showing all 4.6 billion years is neat, but not the point.

* 4.6 billion stays first under Select years, one tap away.
* The Earth in Time is one size on the seven one-row spans: as large as it comfortably can be with the line, both flags, the handle and the events' names in sight at 1,024 × 768 and at iPad size. Say the size at each.
* On the 4.6 billion only, the Earth shrinks as far as it must for the line to be in sight at 1,024 × 768. At iPad size, say what still falls under the fold.

## A detent, in place of Reset and Align North

Michael asked for Align North because it is easy to lose one's bearings turning the globe two ways at once. Reset and Align North were solving the same problem, and they undid each other. Both go, with their code.

In their place:

* North up is a resting place. When she lets go of the globe within about 10° of upright, it eases the rest of the way, quickly and softly. While she drags near upright, it pulls a little toward it. Nothing is said; she may notice and use it. Offer the angle and the strength behind a bench switch, for Michael to feel on the iPad.
* The opening size is a resting place too. When she pinches or scrolls out past the size the Earth opened at, it eases back to that size. Zooming in is free.
* The opening views of step 9 are tilted (Lake Victoria 1.3°, the Alps 46°). Open them upright from now on, each at its own longitude.
* On the map, nothing changes.

## The World

Dark. The World's ground is near-black, not parchment. Partly to mark it as another mode; partly because the lab will one day show the time before the Earth (star and planet formation), and that ground is black.

* The Earth sits in the dark, lit as it is now.
* The ground under the timeline is dark and translucent, fading in as in step 9. The line, the flags, the handle, the years and the events' names are in parchment and pale copper on it.
* The panel keeps its parchment, as the house's panels do.
* Time keeps its parchment. The move between the views fades the ground from one to the other.

A few quiet words. The World has no menu now, so Map / Globe and Full screen work only from Time. Give The World the essential ones, and keep them quiet:

* upper left: Time (as built), and back after Pick a time;
* upper right, small and faint: Map / Globe, then Full screen.

Nothing else. Show cities and the other Information words stay in Time; what they turn on shows in both views. Turn on step 9's fade (`?fade=1`) for these words in The World by default, and say how it feels. The year on the handle never fades.

## Cities as points

With Show cities on, each city is a single small point, with no name showing. A tap on the point shows its name beside it; another tap, or a tap elsewhere, hides it. The same in both views and on the map. Points stay on their ground as it moves through time, as the cities do now.

## The year on the handle

An event dated 4 July 1054 (store year 1054.5) reads 1055 on the handle. Every event in the second half of its year reads a year late. Write the calendar year as the year the event falls in, not the year rounded. Find every place the page rounds a year this way, and say where.

## Ice on today's Earth

Today's globe shows no ice on Greenland or Antarctica, and what looks like ice on the Himalaya. Find out why: the Blue Marble picture, the ice layer, the heights, or something else. Make the present show ice where there is ice today, and say what you changed. Check at 20,700 years too, so the ice of the last cold is still right.

## Building the 4.6 billion (Claude's suggestion; behind a bench switch, off unless Michael asks)

Michael wonders whether the whole 4.6 billion could arrive as a moment of its own, built tile by tile as she moves back, rather than simply appearing.

Behind `?build=1`, try this. When she goes from a smaller span to the 4.6 billion, the mosaic she was on stays where it is, and the older tiles assemble above it from now back to the beginning: a few tiles at first, then faster and faster, with a year count running back beside them, until the last tile lands at the Sun's birth. The first time in a visit it takes about six seconds; after that, the step 7 move. A tap at any point finishes it at once. Reduced motion: a cut. The shuffle's sound, if she has it on.

Say what it looks like, and whether six seconds feels long or short.
