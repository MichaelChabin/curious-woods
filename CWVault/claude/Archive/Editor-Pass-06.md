# Editor pass 6

## The page for Michael

**What was read.** The ten events the daily run drafted on 16 November, in two files: *Coelacanth*, *Monarch winter*, *Sequoyah*, *Belitung wreck* and *Budj Bim* in the first, and *Sobral eclipse*, *Ozone hole*, *Oranjemund wreck*, *Jikji* and *Dufuna canoe* in the second. No events were drafted on the 17th or 18th, so ten is the whole week.

**The verdict.** All ten pass, and none is dropped. Eight are changed, most by a few words; *Oranjemund wreck* and *Dufuna canoe* go through untouched. The daily run's checkers did their work well: I found no wrong number, name or date in any More. The faults I did find are of the kind a checker of facts does not look for. There are five real ones.

- *Budj Bim* puts the place in "south-western Australia" in its summary. It is in the south-west of Victoria, which is the south-east of Australia. The More has it right, and so does the title of the event's own first reference. A child in Perth would have looked for it on her own coast.
- *Budj Bim* again: its date line says "about 6,500 years ago". By the sum in your dates ruling, 4600 BCE and 2026 make 6,626, and the More itself says the charcoal is about 6,600 years old. Both lines now say 6,600.
- *Coelacanth* says "Fourteen years passed before a second was caught", and in the next sentence that fishermen in the Comoros brought in two or three most years. Both cannot be true as written. The fishermen had been catching the fish all along; what took fourteen years was for J. L. B. Smith to see one. It now says so.
- *Belitung wreck* says about sixty thousand pieces were inside, that a fifth had broken, and that the ship therefore loaded seventy thousand. I did the sum three times and could not make it come out, and she will not either. It now says divers brought up about sixty thousand and that many others had broken. The same event had the ship built in "the Near East", which is near only from Europe; it is now "Arabia".
- *Sequoyah* tells the demonstration with his daughter and leaves out its ending: one of them left the room while the other wrote down what was said. The point is that the one who left came back and read it. Five words put that right.

**Smaller things in her text.** Each is in *Changes* below.

- Names used before they were explained: *ejidos* (Monarch winter), *the Nation* and *Tahlequah* (Sequoyah), *Baoli*, *through-beams* and *gilt* (Belitung), *basalt*, *the paper* and *the World Heritage list* (Budj Bim), *the deputy for Sobral* and *glass plates* (Sobral eclipse). Jonathan Shanklin had no employer (Ozone hole). Gutenberg's Bible was not said to be printed from metal type, which is the whole reason for naming him in *Jikji*.
- Pronouns and sentences I read twice: "The next day" in *Coelacanth*, which could be the day after the catch or the day after the skinning; "what they could do" in *Sequoyah*; the sentence in *Belitung wreck* about when the bowl was made and when the ship sank; "That is a smallest age" in *Budj Bim*.
- Where the reader lives: *Sequoyah* described one of his signs as "shaped like our S". A Cherokee child is among the readers, and it is now "the English S".
- A motive given as fact: Fred Urquhart kept the mountain's name out of print because "he thought visitors would wreck it". What is on record is the reason he gave, which was that secrecy would protect the butterflies. The sentence now reports what he said.
- An inference the source does not make: in *Budj Bim*, "the same trap was mended and used for thousands of years". The papers date the channel and, much later, the walls. They do not show unbroken use between. The sentence now says only that the trap was being rebuilt thousands of years after its channel was cut.

**Decided, which you may want to overrule.**

1. *Budj Bim* loses its sentence on eels smoked in hollow trees. It was already hedged to "recent and argued", it is one researcher's idea, and it was competing with the channel and its date, which is the beat. Timeline-Stories asks that a side story be cut when it does that. The National Museum of Australia states the smoking as fact, so if you want it back, it should come back as its own sentence with Heather Builth's name on it. The More is now 335 words.
2. *Belitung wreck* keeps both views on where the ship was built, Michael Flecker's and Stephen Haw's, as the daily run left it and asked you about. I thought hard about our rule that a lone contrarian does not get a sentence. Haw's paper is in a refereed journal, and its target is the weakest link in the chain, the naming of the wood. I searched and found no published reply. So I let it stand as an open argument, and gave Haw his trade ("a historian of China") so that she can weigh him. If you would rather have Flecker alone, the More has room for nothing else in that place.
3. *Sequoyah* now says the immersion school is in "Tahlequah, Oklahoma". The event begins in Alabama and ends in Oklahoma and does not say why. The reason is the removal of 1838, which is not a clause; it is an event of its own, and the store has none. I think it should be written soon, with care, and I have not tried to squeeze it in here.
4. *Sequoyah*'s date line says "about 210 years ago". The plain sum is 205. I left it, since the page will work the figure out for itself, but "about 200" would be the more usual rounding.
5. *Sobral eclipse*: Vicente Saboya is now "a politician elected for Sobral". Nobody has confirmed whether he sat in the state's assembly or the country's, so I could not use the exact word.
6. The first file counts the word *More* in its summary counts and the second does not. The new batch will count all ten the second way, without it.

**Claims I opened a source for** (19 Nov 2026).

- *Ozone hole*, "in 2026 it was the largest in ten years". NASA's own page on the 2026 hole: seventh largest in 48 years, 27.1 million square kilometres on 18 September, and Paul Newman of NASA is reported there as saying it grew larger than any in a decade. It holds. NASA says recovery "by the 2070s"; the More's "around 2066" is the WMO's figure, and the Watch line carries both.
- *Coelacanth*, the Comorian fishermen. The Natural History Museum says the fish was called gombessa locally and that two or three were caught each year by night fishing. That settles which of the two sentences was wrong.
- *Monarch winter*, Urquhart's reason. Journey North's account, drawn from Lincoln Brower: he refused the location even to Brower, and gave as his reason that secrecy would protect the butterflies.
- *Budj Bim*, where it is and the listing. The National Museum of Australia: south-western Victoria; inscribed 6 July 2019; the first Australian place listed purely for its Aboriginal cultural values; at least 6,600 years.
- *Dufuna canoe*, "the oldest boat found in Africa". Wikipedia's article and the Nigerian press still describe it so, and I found no report of an older one. Both have it in Damaturu.
- *Sequoyah*, the school. The Cherokee Nation's own page: Tahlequah, with a second school at Greasy, and all teaching in Cherokee.
- *Jikji*, the present. The last ministerial word I could find on a loan is from 2021, when France said it would consider one. Nothing says the book has moved.
- *Belitung wreck*, a reply to Haw. None found.

**Resting on my own knowledge.** That Smith flew to the Comoros in December 1952 and saw the second fish himself; that Stephen Haw is a historian of China; that Baoli is a reign name, the way years were named in China; that the World Heritage list is kept by a body of the United Nations; that Shanklin worked for the British Antarctic Survey; that "Sobral" is said so-BROW; that Gutenberg's Bible was printed from metal type. Everything else in the ten Mores was read against its own notes and Checked line, and nothing else surprised me enough to open a source.

**Three drawn at random** (shuf, 1 to 10): *Dufuna canoe* (10), *Sequoyah* (3), *Ozone hole* (7). *Dufuna canoe* is unchanged. *Ozone hole* changes in three small places. *Sequoyah* is one of the three most edited, with *Budj Bim* and *Belitung wreck*, so it is the one to read if you read one.

**The spread.** By time: four from the last 150 years (1919, 1938, 1975, 1985), two from 1450 to 1880 (probably 1533, about 1821), two from 1200 BCE to 1450 (about 830, 1377), and two from before 1200 BCE (about 6200 and about 4600 BCE). By place: Brazil, South Africa, Mexico, Antarctica, Namibia, the Cherokee Nation, Indonesia, Korea, Nigeria and Australia. None of the ten is in Europe, and two are shipwrecks.

**What the store still lacks.** With these ten the store will hold 119. Before them it had fifteen events since 1880 and nothing at all between 1923 and 1960; these four help, but the years from 1939 to 1959 are still empty. It has nothing from Russia but *Tunguska*, nothing from Arabia, Persia or Central Asia, nothing from mainland South-East Asia, and little from Africa south of the Sahara. Australia has *Budj Bim* and nothing else. And the Cherokee removal, as above.

## Left alone, on purpose

- *Sequoyah*: "about 210 years ago", and the letters "from English, Greek and Hebrew books". The second is the usual account and is already told as an argument.
- *Monarch winter*: the hired man has no name. Catalina Trail's own account gives his work and not his name, and the event should not guess.
- *Belitung wreck*: "takes the *Afzelia* for African kinds" is a grown-up turn of phrase, and she can manage it.
- *Jikji*: "Korea", not "South Korea" or "Goryeo". In 1377 it was neither of the countries on today's map, and "Korea" is the word that is true of both dates. The last paragraph gives each side its reason, and I left it.
- *Oranjemund wreck*: Kapaandu Shatika "worked for" Namdeb. The sources disagree on his job, so the exact word is not available.
- *Oranjemund wreck* and *Dufuna canoe*: neither names the people who lived on that coast or beside that lake at the time. No source names them, and the events say only what is known.
- *Ozone hole*: it does not say where the paper was published. The journal's name would be one more name she has not met.
- *Sobral eclipse*: Eddington is not named. The plates that carried the result were Sobral's, and the event is Sobral's.
- The pronunciations of Sobral, Oranjemund, Heungdeok, Cheongju and Jikji are fair and unsourced. None for Budj Bim or Tae Rak, rightly, until Gunditj Mirring give one.
