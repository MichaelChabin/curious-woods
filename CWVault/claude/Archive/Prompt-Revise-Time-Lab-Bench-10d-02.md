## The tree of periods, behind a switch

The lab has twenty-six periods of its own. The Time Machine has thirty, each with a checked More (Editor pass 9, 21 Nov), and they form a tree: a coarse period opens into finer ones. The lab's mosaics are nested the same way. Michael wants to see the tree on the two deep-time mosaics before he rules on which periods the lab keeps.

**The switch.** A bench switch in the Time view's *How this works*, *the periods*: the lab's / the tree, and `?periods=tree`. The page opens on the lab's, as now. Nothing of the lab's own periods is removed in this step.

**Humans, Civilization and the four mosaics cut from Civilization are unchanged** by the switch. The tree barely reaches them.

**What a period carries, with the tree on:**

- Its tiles on the mosaic, in its colour. A tile is a million years, so say what you did with the edge at 5.3 Ma.
- Its panel: the Time Machine's label, its span and length, and its summary as the panel's words. The lab's limit for a period's words is 30; the summaries run 20 to 40. Use them whole, and list any that do not fit the panel.
- Its More, built as Dinosaurs' is now.
- The small period name above an event's panel follows the switch: on Complex life it is the leaf the event falls in, on the 4.6 billion the coarse period.
- *select*, the lift of a period's tiles, and a drag across the tiles work as they do now.

**Colours.** If the Time Machine gives its periods colours, start from those. Fifteen in a row on Complex life must still read as fifteen: no two neighbours alike, and nothing that fights the copper and parchment. Say what you chose and where it came from. This is the part Michael most needs to see.

**Not in this trial:** a coarse period opening into its finer ones when she selects it. It is what the tree is for, and it waits for step 11, which changes *select*.
