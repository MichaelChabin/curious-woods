## Arriving in The World

What Michael sees now: a light screen; then the Earth with cities showing; then *How This Works*; then the trilobites' panel opens just above the event's mark. Too much, in the wrong order.

What he wants:

1. **Dark from the first paint**, and the Earth. No light screen first. If the Earth cannot be ready at once, the dark waits for it.
2. **No cities.** *Show cities* is off when the page opens. The Earth is never shown at today on the way in.
3. ***How This Works* open at the upper left**, as it is.
4. **The handle starts near *First trilobites*, not on it.** How near is yours to choose; say what you chose.
5. **The handle flashes once**: light, then dark, then its own copper. This is not meant to be garish. It is just enough to say *here I am*. If the handle and the Earth are both moving, that may be enough by itself.
6. **The handle eases onto the event, and the Earth eases with it**: through the years between, and turning so the event's place faces her. They arrive together. Under reduced motion, a cut.
7. ***First trilobites* is selected, and its panel stays closed.** She should be able to see which event she is on without a panel. Say how it shows. A tap on the event opens its panel, as always.
8. **The place is marked on the globe, if the event has one.** A small mark, with no label. It must not be mistaken for a city's point or for one of her own markers. It rides its plate, as they do.

(The chat's default: the same mark appears for any selected event that has a place, not only at arrival. Say if that costs anything.)

## One panel, in both views

Michael: too many panels open at once. From now on an event, a period and a More share one place.

- **Where.** At the upper right, its top level with the top of *How This Works*. All of it in sight, at every size you check.
- **An event or a period** opens there.
- ***More* replaces the panel** it was tapped in. It does not open beside it. It stands in the same place and may be larger; it must still be wholly in sight.
- **Another event or period** replaces whatever is there, panel or More.
- **Closing a More closes everything.** The event's panel does not come back.
- **If she has moved the panel**, the next one opens where she left it.
- This holds in Time and in The World alike.
- The stepping aside built in steps 10b and 11 (clear of *How This Works*, of *Measure*, of *select*) goes where this makes it needless. Say what is left of it, and where *Measure* now opens.

## The zoom

**Out.** In The World, when Michael zooms out and lets go, the Earth waits two or three seconds and then goes back to its opening size. That is step 10's resting place for size, and it reads as a fault. Take it out. She zooms out as far as the lab lets her, and the Earth stays there. Say what the smallest size is. North's resting place stays. (That the floor alone is enough is the chat's default.)

**In.** Michael: it makes no sense to zoom in further than the picture can bear. Far in is right on the satellite photograph, which is stunning. It is wrong on a reconstruction, where we show rough positions.

- Set the nearest zoom by what the Earth is showing at the handle's year: far in where it is the photograph, less far where it is a reconstruction.
- You know the grades of knowing and where each begins. Propose the limits, show a picture at each, and say what you chose.
- If she is zoomed in further than a year allows and the handle takes her there, the Earth eases out to that year's limit. (The chat's default.)

## The shallow seas and the crust under them

The seas are no longer garish. But now the flooded land is hard to tell from dry land.

- **Try the flooded continental crust in a light grey**, with the faint blue of the water over it, in place of the land's own picture showing through. Dry land keeps its picture.
- Later the lab will drain the oceans, and the deep sea floor will be a darker grey. Choose a grey that leaves room for that. Draining is not in this step.
- **Soften the shorelines**, where shallow sea meets dry land and where it meets deep ocean, if it can be done cheaply. The tears stay straight and sharp; Michael ruled on 21 Nov that they read, rightly, as an abstract view. (Keeping the tears sharp is the chat's caution. - Give him bench switches: the crust *as now / light grey*, and the shorelines *sharp / soft*. Open on light grey and soft.

## Two faults to find. Find the cause and report; mend only the second.

**The coastlines break off.** With *Show coastlines* on, the lines are incomplete. Michael's two cases:

- **639 Ma**, where they are easiest to see: outlines that stop partway round a piece of land.
- **201 Ma**, around the ground of North America, where the Great Lakes show in the land's picture: the line and the blue sea do not agree, and he suspects two sets of data.

He asks: is it that we don't know them, or is something hiding them? The chat's guess is neither: the lines are today's coastlines carried back on their plates, so they were never the old shores, and they break where today's coast lies on crust that was not there yet. Say what the lines are, why they break, and whether anything is drawn over them. Show a picture of each case. **Change nothing here.** Michael will rule on what *Show coastlines* is for once he knows.

**A panel dragged to the top.** When Michael drags a panel all the way to the top of the window, the screen goes grey, as if everything on the page were selected, and the page looks zoomed far in. To get out he has to click inside an open text panel and then zoom out. Make it happen, find the cause, and mend it. At the least:

- a panel cannot be dragged out of the window;
- a drag of a panel never selects the page's text;
- a pinch or a wheel over a panel never zooms the page itself.

Say which browser and which device you could make it happen on. If you cannot make it happen, say so and say what you tried.
