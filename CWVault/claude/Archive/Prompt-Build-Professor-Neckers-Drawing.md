# Build prompt: Professor Necker's Drawing

> Read `CWVault/claude/What-CW-Is.md`, then `cw-deploys/MANIFEST.md` (Page standard), then `CWVault/claude/Rulings-Sept-2026.md`, `CWVault/claude/Story-Voice.md` and `CWVault/claude/Story-Pattern.md`. Then read the story: `CWVault/claude/Story-Professor-Neckers-Drawing.md`, all of it, including *Words on the page* and the notes. Then open `CWVault/claude/mockups/professor-neckers-drawing-mockup.html`: it is not the page and not the standard, but its drawings are right and its geometry is the one to use.
>
> **1. Assets first.** Into `cw-deploys/art/` (three files are already there: `necker-letter-1832-pp336-337.png`, `crystal-calcite.jpg`, `crystal-fluorite.jpg`, all Michael's, no licence needed). Fetch four photographs from Wikimedia Commons, choosing files whose licence is CC0, public domain, CC BY or CC BY-SA, and record for each the file name on Commons, the author and the licence:
> - `crystal-alum.jpg`: start with `File:Potassium_alum_octahedral_crystal.jpg`; it should show a clear octahedron.
> - `crystal-diamond.jpg`: start with `File:Rough_Diamond.jpg`; if it is not a clear octahedral crystal, search Commons for "diamond octahedron" and take the clearest.
> - `crystal-salt.jpg`: search Commons for "halite cube"; one cleavage cube on a plain ground.
> - `crystal-sugar.jpg`: search Commons for "rock candy" or "sucrose crystals"; visible crystal faces.
> Resize each to 1200 px on the long side, JPEG quality 85. Add an `images:` block to the story's frontmatter listing all seven photographs with source, author and licence (Michael's three: "Michael Chabin, 2026"). If a suitable file cannot be found for one crystal, leave that step of the stack without a photograph and say so in the report; do not substitute a diagram.
>
> **2. The page.** Copy `cw-deploys/template-story.html` to `cw-deploys/experiments/professor-neckers-drawing.html` and fill it from the story. The look is `css/story.css`; the page sets only `--aspect`. `placement: beside`. Pictures sit level with the paragraph that names them, never above their section heading, and scroll with the text; the text never says where a picture is. Headings run over the text column.
>
> **3. The drawings.** One small program, on a shelf as `js/necker.js` so the next story can use it; no animation, nothing recorded, no counters. Bare ink lines of even weight, the ink colour from story.css. A parallelepiped is a front face (four points) plus an offset for the back face; draw all twelve edges. Use the mock-up's geometry: the box is front face BL (14,220) BR (114,220) TR (142,112) TL (42,112) in a 240 box with the back face offset (+82,−48); the cube is a 140 square offset (+60,−60). The depth direction must differ clearly from the slant of the front face or the figure goes flat (the notes say why). Five instances, in this order down the page:
> - Picture 1, the box, beside the opening: 225 px, the only finger-sized figure. No controls.
> - Picture 4a, the box with letters, beside "He told Brewster the three things": 135 px; a bold serif A directly above the front middle corner with its foot on the lines, a bold X directly below the back middle corner with its top nearly touching, both in the copper the dots use, a size larger than body text.
> - Picture 5, the filled box, beside "A filled face does the same thing": 135 px; tap cycles A → B → C → A, where A fills the front face opaque (paper, then a quiet wash) so the back middle corner is hidden, B fills the back face so the front middle corner is hidden, C is bare. Immediate, not animated. Caption: "Tap the filled drawing."
> - Picture 6, the Necker cube, beside *The Necker cube*: 135 px, plain.
> - Picture 7, the cube with two copper dots, one on each middle corner, beside "The cube has a copper dot at each of the two middle corners": 135 px, no controls.
> Each figure carries a text description saying what its two readings are.
>
> **4. The crystals stack**, `js/stack.js`, beside "It happened with all sorts of crystals." Five steps, and round again: salt, alum, diamond, calcite, sugar. Each step is the bare-line drawing (salt: the cube; alum and diamond: an octahedron in the cube's oblique view, the middle square as a parallelogram with one point straight above its centre and one straight below; calcite: the box; sugar: an upright block with a slanted depth, from the mock-up) above the photograph, with the caption from the story's *Words on the page*. The bold word at the end of the caption moves the stack (*Next: alum*); no tap on the picture. If a photograph is missing, the step shows the drawing alone.
>
> **5. The letter.** The word *See the letter* in the left column, level with "Tap *See the letter*." Tapping opens `art/necker-letter-1832-pp336-337.png` at full column width in the picker window Glass Geometry uses (backdrop, drag handle, close), with the caption "*The London and Edinburgh Philosophical Magazine*, November 1832, pages 336 and 337." Any other action closes it.
>
> **6. The map and the World.** The margin map is `switzerland` with Geneva in copper, beside "In 1832 Professor Necker had drawers full of crystals"; its own caption; it opens nothing. *Necker's World* comes after *More*, before *References*, built with `js/timeline.js` as the painting pages are: span 1812 to 1852, his side above, the world below, map crop `britain-and-geneva` (8°W–12°E, 44°N–59°N), Tambora and Edo as cards on the east edge. Draw the world events from `stories/world-events.json` and `stories/places.json` by `ref` where they exist (Tambora, Mary Shelley at Geneva, the Great Wave, Faraday, the Beagle, Daguerre); add any that do not yet exist to the shared files in the shared voice, with short labels (*Faraday's generator*, not a sentence). His own events stay in the story: Travels in Scotland (1821), the letter (1832, the focus; its panel: "Mostly about Mont Blanc at sunset. The drawing was the last thing in it."), the clinometer (1832), Le règne minéral (1835), moves to Skye (1841). Brewster's kaleidoscope (1817) and Wheatstone's stereoscope (1838) go to the shared list as world events. The full event list with years and places is in `CWVault/claude/Story-A-Very-Strange-Cube.md` under *Necker's World*.
>
> **7. More.** Seven entries, one paragraph each, opening with a bold lead ending in a full stop. Beside *The small pleasure*: the brain, one call, cutaway, VTA and hippocampus lit with the connection drawn, *again* only. Beside *Other drawings do this too*: a stack of four drawings (Schröder's staircase, the duck-rabbit, Rubin's vase, the three-line corner), bold word moves it. Beside *Alum*: `crystal-alum.jpg` again, own caption.
>
> **8. Remember.** *I want to Remember this* obeys the Maya flag: absent, it fades in beside the last paragraph and puts this cube in the practice list; if the practice queue does not exist yet, leave the inscription and its sentence out and say so in the report.
>
> **9. Icon and gallery picture.** The gallery picture is the Necker cube drawn as a mark, twelve lines with one middle corner in copper, square, at `art/necker-cube-gallery.png`; the tab icon is a 256-px square PNG of the same at `art/necker-cube-icon-256.png`. Frame: a grey-blue close to the ink; size medium. Do not add the page to `stories/gallery.json`; Michael reads it at its own address first.
