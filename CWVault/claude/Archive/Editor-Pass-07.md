# Editor pass 7

## The page for Michael

**What was read.** Deep-time events, batch 3 (ten events, the little balls of Weng'an to the map at 500 million years) and batch 4 (eleven, the first plants on land to the cracking of Pangaea). Every label, summary, More, field, reference, note and Checked line.

**The verdict.** Both batches stand once the fixes below are in. The ages are good. I found no slip between million, billion and thousand, no age that disagrees with its source, and no edge that disagrees with the chart. All 21 are inside the counts, before and after. Nineteen of the 21 change, most by a word or a clause. There are seven real faults and one habit.

- *The Great Dying* calls foraminifera "tiny animals". They are single cells, and not animals. It matters here more than it would elsewhere, because *Little balls*, in the batch before, turns on exactly that difference.
- *The Great Dying* also says a team's computer model "worked out" that the tropical seas warmed by eleven degrees. I opened the paper. The eleven degrees were put into the model, to match what fossils show; what the model worked out was the loss of oxygen and the pattern of the dying. And "where it was worst" hid the surprise: the model's dying is worse far from the equator. The sentence now says what was done.
- *Charnia* says that about 539 million years ago "fossils of shells and bones begin to fill the rocks". Bones come much later. It is now "shells and other hard parts", as *Animals, kept* has it.
- *Gaskiers* says twice that the rock at Mistaken Point holds "the oldest large fossils". Large fossils a billion years older are known, and our own *Mistaken Point* says only "among the oldest large living things" and "the oldest fronds". *Gaskiers* now says fronds.
- *Land egg* had a reference that read "(title to confirm)", which would have gone on the page as it stood. The title is found and is in. The same More says that mammals belong to a group "whose eggs carry their own water", which a ten-year-old will read as a claim that mammals lay eggs. The sentence now speaks of reptiles and birds, which is all the story needs.
- Two events leave it unclear who made the find. In *Mistaken Point*, "he found them" follows two men's names; it was Shiva Balak Misra. In *Dinosaurs*, "he found a skeleton" follows Ricardo Martínez and Paul Sereno; it was Martínez, the Argentine, and the pronoun could have handed his find to the visitor from Chicago. Both now use the name.
- *Little balls* names the Swedish team's leader and calls the team that answered her "a Chinese team". It is now "a team led by Yin Zongjun". The same More ends on "a fossil of the same creature, older and bigger", where "older" was meant as "more grown up" and reads, in a story about ages of rock, as the opposite.
- The habit: "scientists" seven times. Each is now the exact word or a name.

**Smaller things in her text.** Each is in *Changes* below.

- Words that mean something else to a child, or nothing: a "fault in the earth" and "a thousand hectares" (*Coal*); "the Dengying Formation" (*Bites and shells*); "a World Heritage Site", which pass 6 explained in *Budj Bim* and which now reads the same way in *Chengjiang*; "the journal", when no journal had been named (*Jaws*); carbon dioxide pulled from the air "to start the cooling", with no word on why that would cool anything (*Short, sharp ice*).
