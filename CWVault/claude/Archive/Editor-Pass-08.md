## The page for Michael

**What was read.** Deep-time events, batch 5 (twelve events, *Archaeopteryx* to the last glacial maximum), batch 6 (twelve, the oldest beads to Altamira) and batch 7 (three: *Triassic ends*, the revised *Archaeopteryx*, *Hot-country grasses*). **The verdict.** All three batches stand once the fixes below are in. The ages are good. I found no slip between million, billion and thousand, no age that disagrees with its source, and no edge that disagrees with the chart. Batch 6 is the cleanest deep-time batch I have read: four of its twelve go through untouched, and most of the rest change by a clause. Batch 5 needed more. Twenty-one of the 26 events change. There are eight real faults and two habits.

- *Lascaux* says the black paint has "no charcoal in it, so it has never been dated directly". The batch's own Known-from line says "to confirm", and it cannot be confirmed. In March 2026 a team led by Ina Reiche found charcoal in black paint at Font-de-Gaume, another cave in the Dordogne, where everyone had believed there was none, and dated it; the CNRS notice says that no study had ever confirmed the absence of carbon in these caves. The batch knew of the paper and set it aside as "not about Lascaux". It is about exactly the belief our sentence rested on. The More now says the paint "was long thought" to hold no charcoal, and tells the Font-de-Gaume result in one sentence.
- *The asteroid* and *Hot spike* each call foraminifera "tiny animals". They are single cells. The batch's own checkers caught this in *Antarctica freezes* and *Ice by turns*, and say so at the top of the file, but missed the other two. It is the same fault pass 7 found in *The Great Dying*.
- *Ice by turns* ends "Since then there have been about fifty cold spells", straight after a paragraph about 1976. Read as written, there have been fifty ice ages since 1976. It now says "In all". The same More had "And then melt. Then grow again." after a sentence in the past tense, and its summary ended "about fifty cold spells since. We live in a warm one", which makes a warm cold spell.
- *Walking upright* opens: "The Djourab is a desert of dunes and flat, wind-scoured ground. It drives the sand along the ground." "It" was meant to be the wind, which the sentence before never names.
- *Triassic ends* never says the word dinosaur in its More. The footprints grow "a fifth larger", and they are "the biggest meat-eaters' prints", but she is not told whose, nor thirty thousand years from what. It also says "In that band" two sentences after the band it means, with the Blue Lias in between.
- *Ishango bone* ends: "A second notched bone was found at Ishango in 1959. It is in Brussels too." Nothing before it says where the first bone is. The notes say the Congo's rule by Belgium is mentioned "because it is why the bone is in Brussels", so the sentence that was to say so was lost in the cutting. It now says both bones are kept in Brussels.
- *Flowers*: "One of the plants was named after Lluís Marià Vidal… It was named *Montsechia*." I read that twice, and she would too. The plant is *Montsechia vidalii*, named for the mountains and for the man, and the More now says that. Its summary said "Their pollen… is older still", which gives the old pollen to the very plants the More says nobody has matched it to.
- *Antarctica freezes* carried wrong counts in its headings. Your ruling of 11 October put "fifty-five to seventy" in twice, and nobody recounted: the summary is 39 words, not 37, and the More 349, not 347. Both are inside the limits.
- The first habit: "experts" and "scientists". "The experts" twice in *Altamira*, "fossil experts" in *Flowers*, "scientists" in *Archaeopteryx* and *Last glacial maximum*. Each is now the exact word.
- The second habit: Neanderthals named as if she had met them, in *Flutes*, *El Castillo* and *Homo sapiens*. Nothing on the deep line explains them. Two now carry a clause, "another kind of human". The third lost the word; see below.
