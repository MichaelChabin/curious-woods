# Six windows, step by step

Coordinates are in units, 0 at (0, 0) and 1 at (1, 0), y up. *Circle c through p* is centred on c with its edge through p. *Line a b* runs through a and b and on past both. The words after each step are what the demo can say as it draws.

Colours are from the Chartres palette (blue #1a3a8c, red #8b1a1a, amber #c8780a, purple #4a1a8c, green #1a6a2a). On the board each coloured shape must be checked against the pieces the full circles and lines actually make: a shape the look page fills in one go may be several pieces on the board.

## The two first circles (4 steps)

1. Circle (0, 0) through (1, 0). *A circle centered on 0, through 1.*
2. Circle (1, 0) through (0, 0). *A circle centered on 1, through 0. The two circles cross above and below.*
3. Line (0, 0) (1, 0). *A line through 0 and 1. It meets the circles at two new points, one at each end.*
4. Line (1/2, √3/2) (1/2, −√3/2). *A line through the two crossing points. It cuts the middle shape in four.*

**Glass:** The lens: upper half red, lower half amber (the line 0 1 cuts it). The two crescents blue.

## The six-petal rose (7 steps)

1. Circle (0, 0) through (1, 0). *A circle centered on 0, through 1.*
2. Circle (1, 0) through (0, 0). *A circle centered on 1, through 0. It crosses the first circle at two new points.*
3. Circle (1/2, √3/2) through (0, 0). *A circle centered on the upper crossing, through 0. It makes another new point on the first circle.*
4. Circle (−1/2, √3/2) through (0, 0). *A circle centered on that new point, through 0.*
5. Circle (−1, 0) through (0, 0). *Keep going around: centered on the next new point, through 0.*
6. Circle (−1/2, −√3/2) through (0, 0). *And the next.*
7. Circle (1/2, −√3/2) through (0, 0). *And the last. The six petals are finished.*

**Glass:** Inside the first circle: the six petals alternating red and amber, the rest blue.

## The double rose and hexagon (16 steps)

1. Circle (0, 0) through (1, 0). *A circle centered on 0, through 1.*
2. Circle (1, 0) through (0, 0). *A circle centered on 1, through 0.*
3. Circle (1/2, √3/2) through (0, 0). *A circle centered on the upper crossing, through 0.*
4. Circle (1/2, −√3/2) through (0, 0). *A circle centered on the lower crossing, through 0.*
5. Circle (−1/2, √3/2) through (0, 0). *Centered on the new point at the upper left, through 0.*
6. Circle (−1/2, −√3/2) through (0, 0). *Centered on the new point at the lower left, through 0.*
7. Circle (−1, 0) through (0, 0). *Centered on the new point far to the left, through 0. The rose around 0 is finished.*
8. Circle (3/2, √3/2) through (1, 0). *Now around 1. Centered on the new point at the upper right, through 1.*
9. Circle (3/2, −√3/2) through (1, 0). *Centered on the new point at the lower right, through 1.*
10. Circle (2, 0) through (1, 0). *Centered on the new point far to the right, through 1. Two roses, sharing one petal.*
11. Line (−1, 0) (−1/2, √3/2). *Lines for the hexagon: from the far left point to the upper left.*
12. Line (−1/2, √3/2) (3/2, √3/2). *Across the top.*
13. Line (3/2, √3/2) (2, 0). *Down to the far right point.*
14. Line (2, 0) (3/2, −√3/2). *Down to the lower right.*
15. Line (3/2, −√3/2) (−1/2, −√3/2). *Across the bottom.*
16. Line (−1/2, −√3/2) (−1, 0). *And back to where you started.*

**Glass:** Inside the hexagon: all eleven petals red, the two curved triangles between the roses (above and below the shared petal) amber, the rest blue. Michael's own picture of this design was teal, orange and pale blue.

## The twelve-petal rose (20 steps)

1. Circle (0, 0) through (1, 0). *A circle centered on 0, through 1.*
2. Circle (1, 0) through (0, 0). *A circle centered on 1, through 0.*
3. Circle (1/2, √3/2) through (0, 0). *Around the first circle, as for the six-petal rose: centered on the upper crossing, through 0.*
4. Circle (−1/2, √3/2) through (0, 0). *The next one around.*
5. Circle (−1, 0) through (0, 0). *The next.*
6. Circle (−1/2, −√3/2) through (0, 0). *The next.*
7. Circle (1/2, −√3/2) through (0, 0). *The last. That is the first rose.*
8. Line (0, 0) (0, √3). *A line from 0 straight up, through the point high above where two circles cross. It meets the first circle at the top and the bottom.*
9. Circle (0, 1) through (0, 0). *A circle centered on the top point, through 0. It makes two new points on the first circle, halfway between the old ones.*
10. Circle (0, −1) through (0, 0). *The same from the bottom point.*
11. Circle (√3/2, 1/2) through (0, 0). *Now go around again, on the new points: centered here, through 0.*
12. Circle (−√3/2, 1/2) through (0, 0). *The next.*
13. Circle (−√3/2, −1/2) through (0, 0). *The next.*
14. Circle (√3/2, −1/2) through (0, 0). *The last. A second rose, turned halfway between the petals of the first.*
15. Line (1, 0) (1/2, √3/2). *The hexagon: from 1 to the next corner up.*
16. Line (1/2, √3/2) (−1/2, √3/2). *Across the top.*
17. Line (−1/2, √3/2) (−1, 0). *Down the left.*
18. Line (−1, 0) (−1/2, −√3/2). *On down.*
19. Line (−1/2, −√3/2) (1/2, −√3/2). *Across the bottom.*
20. Line (1/2, −√3/2) (1, 0). *And back to 1.*

**Glass:** Inside the hexagon: the first rose's petals red, the second rose's amber, the rest blue. Neighbouring petals overlap; the look page lets the second rose paint over the first, but on the board each overlap is a piece of its own and needs its own colour. The second rose's tips poke just past the hexagon's edges (as in Claude Code's drawing of 4 Nov). The upright line (step 8) cuts the top and bottom petals of the second rose in two.

## A Gothic window (12 steps)

1. Circle (0, 0) through (1, 0). *A circle centered on 0, through 1.*
2. Circle (1, 0) through (0, 0). *A circle centered on 1, through 0. Where they cross is the point of the first small arch.*
3. Line (0, 0) (1, 0). *A line through 0 and 1. It reaches a new point at 2.*
4. Circle (2, 0) through (1, 0). *A circle centered on 2, through 1. That makes the second small arch.*
5. Circle (0, 0) through (2, 0). *A big circle centered on 0, through 2.*
6. Circle (2, 0) through (0, 0). *A big circle centered on 2, through 0. Where they cross is the point of the big arch.*
7. Line (1/2, √3/2) (1/2, −√3/2). *A line through the crossings of the first two circles. It marks the middle of the first small arch.*
8. Line (3/2, √3/2) (3/2, −√3/2). *The same for the second small arch.*
9. Circle (0, 0) through (3/2, 0). *A circle centered on 0, through the middle of the second small arch.*
10. Circle (2, 0) through (1/2, 0). *A circle centered on 2, through the middle of the first. These two cross where the round window will be centered.*
11. Line (0, 0) (1, √5/2). *A line from 0 through that center. It crosses the first circle just below it.*
12. Circle (1, √5/2) through (2/3, √5/3). *The round window: centered there, through that crossing. It just touches every arch around it.*

**Glass:** The two small arches blue, the round window red, what is left of the big arch purple. The two upright lines (steps 7 and 8) cut each small arch down its middle, like a mullion; each half is its own piece.

## The quatrefoil (10 steps)

1. Circle (0, 0) through (1, 0). *A circle centered on 0, through 1.*
2. Circle (1, 0) through (0, 0). *A circle centered on 1, through 0.*
3. Line (0, 0) (1, 0). *A line through 0 and 1.*
4. Line (1/2, √3/2) (1/2, −√3/2). *A line through the two crossings. It cuts the line from 0 to 1 exactly in half.*
5. Circle (1/2, 0) through (0, 0). *A small circle centered on that halfway point, through 0. It crosses the upright line above and below.*
6. Circle (0, 0) through (1/2, 0). *A circle centered on 0, through the halfway point.*
7. Circle (1, 0) through (1/2, 0). *The same, centered on 1.*
8. Circle (1/2, 1/2) through (1/2, 0). *The same, centered on the top of the small circle.*
9. Circle (1/2, −1/2) through (1/2, 0). *And centered on its bottom. Four leaves.*
10. Circle (1/2, 0) through (−1/2, 0). *A circle centered on the halfway point, through the far left of the left leaf. It just touches all four leaves.*

**Glass:** The four leaves blue, the four overlaps between neighbouring leaves red, the rest of the big circle green. The two first circles are only scaffolding here; they cross the design and make pieces of their own.
