# The deep-time periods

## The rule for names

Read each line's names aloud, left to right, and they tell that line's story (Michael, 10 Oct: "a sequence of names that, when read as words, tell the story"). Each line uses one kind of name: what arrived, who ruled, what was new, or the weather. A name is a picture she can hang something on, never a bare temperature or a school word.

The specialists' divisions are kept, because they are useful for the same reason specialists use them (Michael, 10 Oct). Only the names change. The official name sits under each period, small, with what it means: "Phanerozoic: Greek for visible life." Years later, when she meets the official word, she already has the picture.

Nothing makes us the point. Mark Twain's oyster thought the world was made for the oyster. So no period is named *Humans* or *Our line*; people appear as events, and as *Lucy* and *Hand axes* only where the story at that scale really is people and ice.

Every period is at least 5 percent of its line (the tap rule).

## The tree

- **The Earth**: 4.6 billion years to now. Label: *The Earth, 4.6 billion years*.
  - **Molten**: 4.6 to 4.0 billion years ago (Hadean, after Hades, the underworld)
  - **Oceans**: 4.0 to 2.5 billion (Archean, "ancient", from the Greek for "beginning")
  - **Oxygen**: 2.5 to 1.8 billion (early Proterozoic, "earlier life")
  - **Big cells**: 1.8 billion to 720 million (later Proterozoic)
  - **Animals**: 720 million to now. The start is the official start of the Cryogenian, just before the first snowball Earth began (*Snowball one*, 717). The molecular clocks put the first animals between about 800 and 650 million years ago, so the edge is a fair place to begin them, not a date for their arrival; the official Phanerozoic, "visible life", begins at 539. (700, a guess, until 10 Oct; moved so the snowball opens the line rather than straddling the edge.)
    - **Soft bodies**: 720 to 539 million (Cryogenian and Ediacaran)
    - **Trilobites**: 539 to 252 million (Paleozoic, "old life")
      - **Shells**: 539 to 487 (Cambrian, after Cambria, Wales)
      - **Crowded seas**: 487 to 444 (Ordovician, after the Ordovices, a people of Wales)
      - **Green land**: 444 to 419 (Silurian, after the Silures, another)
      - **Fish**: 419 to 359 (Devonian, after Devon)
      - **Coal forests**: 359 to 299 (Carboniferous, "coal-bearing")
      - **One continent**: 299 to 252 (Permian, after Perm, in Russia)
    - **Dinosaurs**: 252 to 66 million (Mesozoic, "middle life")
      - **First dinosaurs**: 252 to 201 (Triassic, "threefold", for three layers in Germany)
      - **Giants**: 201 to 143 (Jurassic, after the Jura mountains)
      - **Flowers**: 143 to 66 (Cretaceous, "chalky")
    - **Mammals**: 66 million to now (Cenozoic, "new life")
      - **Recovery**: 66 to 56 (Paleocene)
      - **Heat**: 56 to 34 (Eocene)
      - **Cooling**: 34 to 23 (Oligocene)
      - **Grass**: 23 to 5.3 (Miocene)
      - **Ice**: 5.3 million to now (Pliocene to now)
        - **Lucy**: 5.3 to 2.6 million (Pliocene)
        - **Hand axes**: 2.6 to 1 million (early Pleistocene; the ice comes and goes about every 41,000 years)
        - **Mammoths**: 1 million to now (the ice ages grow longer, about 100,000 years each)
          - **The last ice age**: 130,000 years ago to now (Late Pleistocene and Holocene). The rest of the Mammoths line is a plain stretch with events.
            - **Hippos in London**: 130,000 to 115,000 (the last warm spell; hippo bones under Trafalgar Square)
            - **Ice sheets**: 115,000 to 11,700 (the last cold spell)
            - **After the ice**: 11,700 to now (Holocene), the door to the Time Machine

## Read across

- *Molten, Oceans, Oxygen, Big cells, Animals*: what arrived.
- *Soft bodies, Trilobites, Dinosaurs, Mammals*: who ruled.
- *Shells, Crowded seas, Green land, Fish, Coal forests, One continent*: what was new.
- *First dinosaurs, Giants, Flowers*: what was new.
- *Recovery, Heat, Cooling, Grass, Ice*: the weather.
- *Lucy, Hand axes, Mammoths*: who you'd meet.
- *Hippos in London, Ice sheets, After the ice*: what you'd see.
