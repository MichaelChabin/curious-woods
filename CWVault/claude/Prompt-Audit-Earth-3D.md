*Two things Michael settled before this was written (26 Oct): the 3D Earth lives on a bench beside the Time Machine until it matches the globe, and only then replaces it; and she reaches the inside with a word, **Cut open**, which takes a wedge out and closes it again.*

*Later the same day Michael ruled on the ground (item 5 below): Blue Marble is the skin, ETOPO the data, the story maps match the globe, and the photograph fades to paint by 2.6 million years.*

*Note: the held Drain the Sea audit has already run (24 Oct, `Audit-Drain-the-Sea-vs-Time-Machine-Oct24.md`), though the worknote of 25 Oct says it hasn't. So this prompt doesn't repeat it. It reads that report and asks only what is new: what Drain the Sea needs from a globe that is round.*

> **The idea being tested.** Today the globe works out each pixel in JavaScript: which plate is here at this age, turn it back to today, read today's picture. The plan is to do the same on the graphics card, as a shader written in TSL on three.js's WebGPU renderer (per the standard): today's picture, a "which plate" picture, and a small table of each plate's turns by age. That would make the globe fast and truly round, so it can be lit, tilted, cut open, zoomed until it flattens into the map, and turned to follow a voyage. The aim is **one Earth object** for the deep-time globe, the story maps' globe, Drain the Sea and the cut-away. Tell me where this plan is wrong before telling me how to do it.
>
> **Report:**
>
> 1. **Everything `globe.js` does.** A plain list of its jobs: the two projections (globe and flat, and the flat view that paints a page's map view), the view rules (north up at rest, the tilt limits, a drag right moves the surface east), the grades and their seam, the skin, the island and shelf, the ice of the cold ages, the molten sequence, the motion model and *another guess*, the limb shading and haze, the hooks a page uses (`placeAt`, `project`, `onTurn`, `onGrade`, `onMolten` and the rest). For each job say: **moves to the graphics card**, **stays in JavaScript**, or **goes away** because a real sphere makes it unnecessary. Name anything that doesn't fit any of the three.
> 2. **Every caller.** Every page that loads `globe.js` (I know of `active/time-machine.html` and `experiments/maps/drain-the-sea-bench.html`; find any others), which parts of its surface each uses, and what each page draws **over** the globe (cities, the probe's ring, labels, the water ball). In 3D, things drawn over a ball must either live in the scene or be placed by projecting from it. Say which each one should be.
> 3. **The "which plate" picture.** Today the id raster is redrawn per age into an equirectangular canvas, at half-million-year steps. On the card there are at least two ways: keep building that picture in JavaScript and hand it to the card when the age moves; or one fixed picture of the plates as they are today, with the card trying each plate's turn per pixel. Say which fits, given how many plates and pieces there are, how many ages are sampled, and how the guessed and invented ages work (the motion model's stepped run, the blobs). Give the numbers: plates, pieces, samples, the size of the table.
> 4. **The motion model.** `runSim` is a stepped simulation with collisions, cached per step and per seed. Confirm it stays in JavaScript and only hands the card a turn per plate per frame, and say whether anything in it assumes the 2D canvas.
> 5. **The ground.** Today's ground picture is `art/maps/world.webp`, coloured by the map's height ramps (Spec-Maps, *The ground colours*). Michael wants NASA's Blue Marble as the measured ground on the globe. Report: the Blue Marble sources, their licences, resolutions and sizes; what the five grades would look like if the photograph is the top grade (per *Honesty in pictures* in the standard); and what it takes to carry out Michael's rulings of 26 Oct: (a) ETOPO stays underneath as the data for height, sea level, coasts, contours and ice thickness, and Blue Marble becomes the skin of today's ground, in place of the height-coloured ramps and the vegetation layer; (b) the story maps match the globe, so they take the same skin; (c) the photograph is true only near today, so it fades to paint by the seam at 2.6 million years, where the plates start to move, not at 300 million as the globe's skin does now; the paint follows the colours already ruled (green while there are plants, bare rock before, near-black before oxygen). Say what the photograph does to the legibility of labels and vermilion marks on a story map, and which month (or months) of Blue Marble to use. Name what in Spec-Maps these rulings would change.
> 6. **Close in, the map.** Far out the Earth is a globe; close in it should flatten into the map's detail. Say how the map's tile pyramid (`art/maps/world-tiles/`, levels 0 to 6, equirectangular WebP, with ice and vegetation layers and contours per column) could feed the sphere at close zoom: tiles drawn onto the ball, or a handover to `map.js` at some zoom, or something better. Say what the handover would look like to her, and what happens to the Mercator-to-equirectangular fade of *The world that moves*. Michael's thought (26 Oct): a story map may simply be the one Earth at the zoom the story chooses. Van Gogh's Provence opens close in and looks flat; Hokusai's blue, a pigment from Berlin, wants the globe, because its route runs half way round the world. Say whether that makes the regional still crops unnecessary, and what it costs a story page that today loads one small picture.
> 7. **What Drain the Sea needs from a round globe.** Building on the 24 Oct audit, not repeating it: the sea-level layer (lowering the sea on a ball, from the same height data), the floor's age and the stripes as layers on the ball, the probe on a sphere (one finger turns the ball in 3D, per the standard; one finger moves the probe in Drain the Sea: say where they meet), the depth gauge, and the water ball as a real sphere at true scale beside the Earth. Say which of these are the Earth's and which are the story's.
> 8. **The inside.** What the *Cut open* word needs: the Cut Open sketch's partial sphere and two cut faces, its cut-face drawing, the scale note, and how the molten sequence and the early crust on the outside would line up with the inside at the same age. Which of the standard's shelf pieces the Earth needs first (`scene.js`, `cutaway.js`, `noise.js`, `glow.js`, `honest.js`, `still.js`), and whether any of them exists yet.
> 9. **The standard.** Whether three.js r186 is in the site yet; what the WebGL 2 fallback costs this Earth; what the Earth's tiers would turn down; and the one thing that must survive at the floor tier (my guess is the plates moving, but say if it's something else). Any place where the 2D globe does something the standard forbids, or the standard asks for something the globe has never done.
> 10. **An honest cost.** How many Claude Code sessions, how big the new files and textures, what the repo grows by, the risks, and what can only be judged by Michael's finger on the iPad. Say what you are guessing.
> 11. **An order.** A rough order of work that starts with a bench in `experiments/` beside the Time Machine and ends with the Time Machine on the 3D Earth, with every page working at every step. Rough is fine; the spec will settle it.
