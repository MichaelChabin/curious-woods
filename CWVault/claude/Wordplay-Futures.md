# Wordplay, later

Wordplay began as an easy entertainment and has turned into a way for a child to explore poetry and, in time, literature: limericks first, then Dickinson, haiku (one day in Japanese), Shakespeare, Li Bai once Maya is in, and prose.

## Other kinds of poems, and prose

- **A family of collections.** Today there is one file, `stories/limericks.json`. Later, one file per collection (limericks, Dickinson, haiku, a prose passage), all in the same shape, and Wordplay reads whichever are hanging.
- **Each record says what kind of thing it is** (`form`: limerick, haiku, quatrain, prose). The form decides the shape: which lines indent (the limerick's 3 and 4), how many pieces it breaks into, and for prose, whether the pieces are sentences or phrases.
- **An Opening for each new poet or form**, as Lear has now; each poet so introduced also gets a gallery story.
- **Copyright** is checked per poet: Dickinson's 1890s texts are public domain, the 1955 ones with her dashes are not; Li Bai needs an old translation or our own.

## What she likes

- **On her device, and nowhere else.** Her list, her titles and her notes are already the record of what she likes. No server, no profile, no counts shown to anyone.
- The page can lean toward more of what she keeps (the same poet, the same form, the same trick) without anything leaving her iPad.
- Kept poems should also appear in the gallery's Saved Stories, with some way of organizing them (Michael, 28 Sept).
- The app can lose its memory (Safari clears a site's storage after about seven days unless it is on the home screen). That is one reason for search.

## Search

- Each record carries a few plain labels: poet, form, places, the tricks it uses, and ease. Search is the page looking through its own files. No server is needed until the pool is in the thousands.
- Searching her own list and the whole pool can be one box.

## Her own poems

- She writes the lines, and they become a puzzle.
- **A puzzle can travel inside a link**: the poem is carried in the address itself, scrambled, so she can send a friend her own limerick to put back together, and nothing is stored anywhere.
- A step between: she changes one word of a real poem and sees whether it still scans (Writing and Editing, in the Foundation's Practice).

## Choices to make now that keep this easy

- The texts live in the data file only; specs hold decisions, sources and checks (done, 29 Sept).
- Keep the record shape plain and add fields rather than change them (`form`, `tags` when they are needed).
- Keep everything about her on her device.
