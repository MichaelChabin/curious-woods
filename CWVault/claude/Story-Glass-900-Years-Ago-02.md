# Glass 900 years ago

In about the year 1100, a man who called himself Theophilus wrote a book named *De diversis artibus*. The title means "the many arts."

The book explains how to make just about anything… how to make paint and the brushes to paint with, how to cast a church bell, and even how to make glue out of cheese.

For this story, the most interesting part of the book tells how stained glass windows were made 900 years ago. At that time, success meant using the best available technology, careful science, and serious art.

Imagine you live in Northern Europe. The year is 1100 and you just turned 12 years old. Your family has been making fine windows for three generations — you'll be the fourth, and you are confident you'll be good at it. After all, you've been helping around the shop and furnace for as long as you can remember. You know quite a bit.

For your birthday, you are being allowed to make your first window.

For the design, you start with a flat board, a little larger than your window will be. You prepare its surface by soaking it with water. Then you rub soft chunks of chalk into the wet surface, until the board is covered with a thick, perfectly smooth layer of white paste, which you let dry overnight.

The next day, the process has left you with a flat white surface perfect for drawing fine lines using a sharpened piece of lead.

You are planning a pattern you call a "double rose and hexagon." It looks like this:

[picture: the double rose and hexagon]

You know that to make this design, you need circles and lines. To be honest, you could make just about anything out of lines and circles.

To make the lines, you'll use a "straight edge." Yours looks like a flat wooden ruler, without the markings. For circles, you use your grandfather's compass. Actually, it is yours now. He gave it to you, but it belonged to his father when he was a boy.

[picture: a compass]

The compass is made of iron, and has two points joined by a stiff hinge. You can put one of the points anywhere you like. That will be the center of the circle. Then, holding that center point in place, you drag the second point across the chalky surface all the way around the center. The result is always a perfect circle.

The compass and straight edge are ancient tools. They've been used for thousands of years to make every kind of drawing, from bridge designs to wall decorations and from city planning to pure mathematical geometry. Millions of different drawings have been made with these two simple devices. Even so, no one has ever made every design possible with them. No one ever will.

Once your design is finished, the next step is to cut brilliantly colored pieces of stained glass to fit every shape in the drawing. Then thin strips of lead are used to fit the glass together. The lead strips are called "came," and when they are soldered into place the window is strong enough to stand on its own. It is finished.

So, if you like, the next step is to make your window.

Below you'll see a white space. That is your board.

You'll also see two copper dots, called "points." They are labeled 0 and 1. You use them to make your first lines and circles. Those will make still more points you can use for more lines and circles.

To make a line, tap one point then another. A line will connect them and extend beyond the edge of the screen.

To make a circle, tap one point and drag to another. A circle will form, centered on the first with its edge through the other.

When your lines and circles cross, you'll have new points that you can use for new lines and circles.

And, of course, you can tap a color then tap any shape you make to color your glass.

On the left side, you'll see a panel labeled "How this works." It is there if you'd like a reminder of how things work, or if you need to undo anything… or if you want to share your window with someone else.
