# The Time lab bench, step 4: after a day with step 3

`cw-deploys/experiments/time-lab.html` only. Read Report-Time-Lab-Bench-03.md first.

## The timeline is always there

Step 1's rule, that the line is absent until the first touch, is withdrawn: after one visit, an empty space where the line belongs reads as broken. The line is on the page from the start, spanning the whole mosaic in use, its flags at the two ends, the handle at today (or at the mosaic's newest tile), the events of the whole span on it as the Time Machine's deepest line draws them. Choosing a scale (*4.6 billion*, *Last million*, *Last 12,000*) resets the line to that whole span. What stays absent until there is a selection narrower than the whole is ***focus***; *back* likewise appears only when there is something to step back from.

## No box on the mosaic

The plaque that appears over the mosaic on pointing and on tapping goes, with its styles. The lift of the period's tiles stays (it shows how long the period is); the text under the Earth does the telling. The rollover label at the cursor (the period's short name, the span while dragging) stays.

## Line one is the thing she touched; the year goes to the handle

- **The handle gets a year label**, set as the flags' labels are, with the word of knowing after it: *252 million years ago · inferred*, the word in copper italic. It follows the handle live. Where the handle's label would collide with a flag's, the handle's wins and the flag's steps aside, as the flags already do for each other.
- **Line one of the text block** no longer carries the year. It carries the name and span of what she touched last: for a period, *Formation · 4,567 to 4,400 million years ago · 167 million years* (name, span counting ago as the flags do, then the length as the box words gave it: *six thousand years*, *150 years, so far*); for an event, its label and date as the summary's first words have them (*The asteroid · about 66 million years ago*). At rest, with nothing touched, line one carries the scale in use (*The Solar System · 4,600 million years*; *The last million years*; *The last 12,000 years*).
- The grade's sentence (what the picture is at this age) leaves the text block; the word on the handle is enough, and the sentence stays available in *How this works*.

## Two columns under line one

Under line one, two columns of the house text, each six lines tall and of fixed height, stacked at iPad width (below 800 px):

- **Left, the period:** the box words of the period selected (or pointed at while the pointer is down), name in bold and then the text, as the block shows them now. Empty, not collapsed, when no period is chosen.
- **Right, the event:** the summary of the event tapped last, ending in *More*, the whole summary a tap as now. Empty until an event is tapped; cleared when the selection changes to a span the event is not in.

Both stay visible at once; neither replaces the other. Six lines at 1,024 wide is about 40 words a column; a longer text is cut with an ellipsis as now, the *More* kept. Say in the report what the block's height is now against step 3's 93 px, and what the Earth lost.

## The last million without numbers

The million row loses its year numbers under the blocks (900,000 … 100,000, *now*), as the 12,000 row did in step 3; the names under the row are its labels, in the same style. *Now* at the right end stays only if the 12,000 row has an equivalent; otherwise it goes too.
