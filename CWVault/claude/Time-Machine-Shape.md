# The shape of the Time Machine

It began as a timeline with a linked map. It is becoming a way to see the past under lenses.
To keep it one thing, everything it shows is one of five nouns, and an author does one of
three verbs. An idea that is not one of the nouns waits.

## Five nouns

**Event.** A point in time, usually with a place. What the store holds today
(`stories/timeline-events.json`, `stories/after-the-ice-events.json`, `stories/world-events.json`).
The label, the summary and the More are Timeline-Stories.md.

**Period.** A span in time, often with a region: the Tang (618–907); a language while it was
spoken; bronze while it was the best metal. On the line, a band; with a region, a wash on the map
that comes and goes with the year. The stretch an uncertain date already draws is this mechanism,
not yet named. *Not built.* A period with a region is the one addition the engine still needs; it
is what languages, technologies and empires are made of.

**Curve.** A quantity by year: people, sea level, cases of cholera. A file in `stories/curves/`
with its source, licence and how sure it is, and a word in the column. Built 30 Sept.

**Layer.** The ground as it was: the sea, the ice, the green Sahara. A file beside the pyramid;
the map turns it on for a year (Spec-Maps, *Time on the map*). The sea is built.

**Viewing.** A zero, a name, a span, and which lenses are on. *After the Ice* is one viewing,
the widest. The Egyptian line, zero at the drying of the Sahara and the end at Cleopatra, is
another. A Chinese line from the traditional beginnings to now, Tang as a band, is a third. Same
engine, same store; a page costs almost nothing. (A timeline is a zero and a name: Spec-Maps,
ruled 27 Sept.)

## Three verbs

Name a zero. Set a span. Turn a lens on. That is all an author does.

## Ruled 1 Oct 2026 (Michael): in a story the timelines are fixed

The author sets the zero and the span so that the child sees the proportion — how much of
Chinese history is Tang — and the Focus window does not move. Only the lab is adjustable. This
settles the lab-and-viewing split: the lab (`active/time-machine.html` today) is where every
capability is on and Michael sees what a creative child could do; a story's viewing is the same
engine with the adjustable parts absent, and the column holds only the words that can act. It
also answers the testing problem: testers get viewings, not the lab.

## Three kinds of page

- **A gallery story** has something to do — Egyptian multiplication, a Chinese character — and
  ends with a World section, which is a fixed viewing.
- **A Time Machine application** is a viewing where the line and the map are the thing to do:
  adjustable, with a question she can record. The Time Machine itself is the first.
- **An event story** lives in the store and surfaces through any viewing that reaches its year.
  It never needs a page of its own.

## After the Ice

It stops being the whole thing and becomes the widest viewing, whose lesson is twelve thousand
years against everything. The tool makes that point once, generally, and then the same point
about Egypt or China at a different zero.

## The test for a new idea

Is it an event, a period, a curve, a layer or a viewing? If not, it waits. If it is, it is a file
in a shape that exists and one word in the column — never a change to the engine for one story.
