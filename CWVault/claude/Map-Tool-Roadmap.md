Twelve Acts

The plane is a place. A tool belongs on this list only if it is something you do to that place. That rule is what keeps this a list of twelve acts rather than a list of forty topics.

Each act serves several ideas. Build the act once; the ideas arrive free.

Tags: [E] exists · [S] specified, unbuilt · [N] new this session.

1 · Make a rectangle

Tap two numbers, or drag out from an axis.

Multiplication · commutativity (one pane, many rectangles) · squaring as tapping one number twice · the equals sign as sameness rather than result · (a + b)² and the other identities, by cutting a side

[E] rectangle anchored at origin, corner snap, reveal [N] tap-twice to square · drag from either axis with a corner handle Needs: nothing structural. The identities need only a draggable cut.

2 · Reshape it — same glass, new shape

Hold the panes; move the corner. The number never changes.

Divisors · area against perimeter (same glass, different came) · the hyperbola · logarithms · e · γ · the tangent by counting

[N] the whole act. This is the only genuinely new mechanism from Aug 19. Needs: a came-length readout that rises while the glass count holds. Payoff: the square always needs the least came — 36 runs 74, 40, 30, 26, 24.

3 · Restack it

Choose a number of stacks. Deal the panes out. Watch the height grow.

Division as the missing side · fractions as complete answers · remainders · the two meanings of division (how tall each stack / how many stacks fit)

[S] Restack Needs: the leftover row readable both ways — 2 left over and two-fourths of a row. Same object. One is arithmetic, the other is modular arithmetic later.

4 · Cut finer

Change the resolution. Halves, then tenths, then hundredths.

Decimals · fractions of fractions · multiplying fractions · the beginning of limits

[N] the slider (0.5, then powers of 10) Note, correcting an earlier claim of mine: multiplying fractions is not a different picture. It is act 1 at a finer resolution. Two thirds of three quarters cuts the square 4 across and 3 down — the cuts cross, so there are 12 pieces, and you keep 3 × 2 = 6. The rule "top times top, bottom times bottom" is the rectangle. See the graphic.

5 · Tile around it

Wrap a border of smaller squares around a square to grow it.

Square roots, digit by digit · the odd numbers (a gnomon of side n is 2n+1) · completing the square · quadratic equations · Pythagoras, given the geometry layer

[S] Make Square Needs: the gnomon count shown as it is laid — 21, 23, 25, 27. Payoff: this is the largest single win on the list. Al-Khwarizmi solved quadratics by cutting and re-tiling exactly this way. School algebra's hardest unit is already sitting in a gnomon.

6 · Peel squares off it

Cut away the largest square that fits. Repeat on what is left.

gcd (the last square) · continued fractions · Fibonacci · the golden rectangle · irrationality, when the peel eats itself forever

[N] Needs: dragged sides, not typed, so she can land on √2 by accident. And a mark when the leftover is the same shape as its parent — that recognition is the proof.

7 · Draw a line through zero

A ray from the origin out across the plane.

Ratio · similarity · slope · every fraction in lowest terms · the orchard, visible and hidden trees · π by counting · Farey neighbours and the mediant (adding two arrows)

[N] Needs: the ray, and a readout of which pane it first strikes.

8 · Light up the panes that…

Choose a test; the panes that pass it glow.

Primes (they glow only on the rails, never the interior) · squares · pronics · triangular · powers · how many rectangles land on this pane, which answers is 1 prime by counting and kills bigger means more factors

[E] this is Properties. New highlights are new Properties entries, not a new mode. Needs: the rectangle-count readout permanently on.

9 · Recolour the whole plane

Every pane takes its colour from one declared rule.

Largest prime factor (the plaid) · gcd (the orchard) · divisor count · factor-parity, which looks like static and is an open question

[E] fog-map, by largest-prime-factor rank Rule: within one viewing, colour means one declared thing. A second rule is a second viewing, never an overlay.

10 · Rewrap the counting

Lay the numbers out n to a row. Drag n.

Clock arithmetic · times tables mod n · cycles and periods · primes crowding into very few columns

[N] — highest value per unit of build cost on this list Needs: only the rewrap and a draggable n.

11 · Count what is there

Not a tool. A permission. Panes and pixels are countable.

Area · π from the orchard · γ from the columns · divisor totals · the honest limit — areas converge, lengths do not

[E] partly Needs: counting always allowed, never pre-answered.

12 · Walk from pane to pane

Step right or up, one pane at a time, from zero to somewhere.

Counting paths · Pascal's triangle · combinations · and the doorway to randomness, since a random walk is this act with the choice taken away

[N] Needs: path drawing and a count of distinct routes. This is the act that the randomness plane will sit on top of.

Where this does not reach

Short list, one line each, so the gaps have names.

Randomness — outcomes that vary, and what stays stable about them anyway.
Direction — quantities that point, and can therefore cancel: vectors, signs, complex numbers.
Function — a rule you can feed, compose, and run backwards, treated as a thing in itself.
Turning — angle, rotation, and anything that repeats: the trigonometric functions.
Symbols — reading mathematics written as notation rather than shown as a picture.
Argument — proof structure as such: induction, contradiction, the obligation to show why. (Partly owned already: act 5 is induction in pictures, act 6 is descent.)

Michael's plan is that music, timeline, and randomness planes sit on top of this one with their own tool sets. That works for the first four. Symbols and argument are not planes and will need another answer.

Suggested build order
Act 10, rewrap — cheapest, and it pays for three of the best patterns.
Act 2, reshape — the new mechanism everything else leans on.
Act 8's rectangle count — one readout, and it silently settles two misconceptions.
Act 5, Make Square — biggest single idea on the list.
Act 3, Restack — already specified; finish it.

Acts 6, 7, 9, 12 are rich but can wait. Act 4's slider should land whenever act 2 does, because it is what pays the multiplication makes things bigger debt.

Naming

Every act above should end up with a word from the Marauder's-Map vocabulary, not a description. Restack and Make Square already have theirs. The others need words before they are built, not after.
