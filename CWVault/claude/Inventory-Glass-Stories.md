# The glass geometry stories

## The idea, stated once

Stories introduce ideas. Each one uses a subset of the lab's tools to do it. So the lab is not designed from a theory of geometry and then given stories; it is designed by thinking through the stories and adding up what they need. Six months ago the question was "how does Euclid lead to the properties of numbers." The question now is "what does the next story need that the last one didn't."

Three rulings from this (Michael, 22 Oct):

**The grid is given.** The plane is ruled from the first story. Nobody builds the grid from circles and lines. This is a deliberate break with tradition; the test is what a ten-year-old can learn, not what the tradition says comes first. Euclid arrives when he is useful (proofs), and one of the first stories about him is what he missed.

**Several stories may do the same work.** The one-door rule is retired. A capability is opened by whichever story she meets first, and more than one story may open it. There are many ways into the coordinate plane and from there into the properties of numbers, and each child should be able to take her own.

**Each story introduces, ideally, one new idea with useful consequences.** Wright introduces parallel lines. That is the one constraint that keeps a story from sprawling.

## The stories

Each entry: what it introduces, what she does on the board, what the lab must have for it, and what is new to the lab at that point. Deliverables are the same everywhere unless a story says otherwise: the window (the postcard), and the plan — a technical drawing with the parts list by colour and came, and possibly costs.

### Wright Windows

*Introduces:* the positive coordinate plane, by way of the frame. Parallel lines.

*On the board.* A frame lives on the plane and snaps to intersections in x and y. She sizes it by dragging its sides. Lines are pulled off any side of the frame, or any edge of any pane, parallel to it, and stop at the lines that bound the pane. Resolution can change to halves. Clear glass is in the palette, because Wright used clear and not Roman green.

*Variants, deliberately allowed later in the story or in a second visit:* lines drawn between intersections, which gives diagonals; circles drawn from one intersection to another, decorative only — their crossings make no new points. Wright used circles.

*The lab must have:* the grid; a frame that snaps to it; resolution; lines pulled from edges; readouts while dragging; clear glass; the plan with inventory.

*New to the lab:* all of it, since this is first. Note that the circles-make-no-points rule is a board setting, not an engine flag: the same gesture on Stained Glass does make points.

*Door:* Wright. The beat is not yet chosen; the Froebel blocks are a candidate.

### Stained Glass

*Introduces:* construction — points found by crossing.

*On the board.* The existing Glass Circles story with new demos. The frame sits on the grid; resolution can change; grid points can be used to start a construction; both circles and lines; intersections create new points. Taking the straightedge away was arbitrary, so it comes back, and it reads better after lines have been met as parallels.

*The lab must have:* everything Wright had, plus lines and circles from point to point, and crossings that make points.

*New to the lab:* crossings as points. Demos for this story.

*Door:* Theophilus, as now.

*Open, kept from the straightedge board when it retired (Michael's ruling 1, 24 Oct 2026; the board and its page are gone, these two ideas are not):* a free point placed outside the frame, once, movable until something is drawn from it; and the construction drawn fainter outside the frame than inside, so the window reads as the window and the rest as scaffolding.

### Triangles

*Introduces:* shapes that grow from shapes.

*On the board.* Start with one equilateral triangle. Tap any third of any side and pull or push: a new equilateral triangle grows on that third, outward or inward. Extend any line in its own direction in steps of its own length. Possibilities: something like the Koch snowflake, hexagons, tiling the plane. The frame is on the grid.

*The lab must have:* a polygon as a unit; grow-on-a-third; extend-by-own-length.

*New to the lab:* shapes as units that beget shapes.

*Door:* not chosen.

### Limits

*Introduces:* a shape inscribed in itself, as far as she likes.

*On the board.* Start with a square. Tap and drag an edge: the square is duplicated, rotates and shrinks until it is exactly inscribed. The new square is half the first. Repeat. The areas are shown in several ways. Any polygon can do it. The results accumulate, so she ends up with a series of geometric limits.

*The lab must have:* inscribe-and-rotate; area shown several ways.

*New to the lab:* the inscribing move. (Michael would love nested pentagons building a golden rectangle.)

*Door:* not chosen.

### Right Triangles, the design version

*Introduces:* fractions as slopes.

*On the board.* On the coordinate plane, a tap anywhere makes a right triangle with its point at the origin, base on the x axis, vertical side at the tap. Dragging the upper right corner resizes it, limited by the resolution. Each triangle is labelled with its height and width along its edges and with h/w as a fraction. When h/w is in lowest terms (3/4), a vertical goes up the right side; from then on h/w = 3/4 is displayed and a vertical drops at each multiple. These pieces can be coloured one by one or in a single colour for the fraction. Mirror mode, or a different design in each quadrant.

*The lab must have:* tap-to-triangle from the origin; corner drag at the resolution; fraction labels; verticals at multiples; mirror and quadrant modes.

*New to the lab:* mirror and quadrant modes.

*Door:* Brunelleschi, shared with the version below. See the fraction-window worknote.

### Right Triangles, the perspective version

*Introduces:* lines as sight lines.

*On the board.* The same right triangles on the plane with dimensions, read as lines in one-point perspective. Paintings and photographs can be shown behind the plane. Maybe horizontals, for the tops of buildings and cubes. Could become a series on perspective.

*The lab must have:* a picture behind the plane, carried by zoom and pan.

*New to the lab:* the picture behind the plane. This is the one shell-level need in the list that no earlier board has; it should be known before the shell is rebuilt.

*Door:* Brunelleschi; Caillebotte (Paris Street; Rainy Day, The Floor Scrapers) for the series.

### Egyptian Rope Stretchers

*Introduces:* a right angle made from a rope with knots (3, 4, 5).

*On the board:* nothing new, if right triangles and the grid exist.

### Ahmose: multiplication as geometry

*Introduces:* 7 × 5 as a rectangle on the plane, five wide and seven high. Multiplication is deciding which rows to keep.

*On the board:* rectangles with dimensions, rows that can be kept or dropped.

*The lab must have:* a rectangle as a unit with its rows. Probably nothing new beyond the Wright panes.

### Euclid

*Introduces:* who he was, and what he missed — the coordinate plane — while Alexandria was being built around him on something close to one.

*On the board:* nothing new. Constructions she already has.

## What the lab is, read off the list

The union of the right-hand needs above is the lab. Grouped:

- **The plane:** endless; the grid given; resolution; zoom, pan, reset; readouts while dragging; a picture behind it.
- **The frame:** a crop; snaps to the grid; sized by its sides; locks only when something has been pulled from it.
- **Panes, by board:** parallel lines from edges (Wright); points, lines and circles with crossings (Stained Glass); shapes that grow from shapes (Triangles); inscribing (Limits); right triangles from the origin (Right Triangles); rectangles with rows (Ahmose).
- **Glass and came:** tap a pane to colour; clear glass; colour decides the came; came by lead or zinc, thickness chosen; mirror and quadrants where a board asks.
- **What she makes:** the window; the plan with its parts list and possibly costs; Share.
- **The column:** How this works, Colours, the finished work and Undo, New Open Save, Share, Reset view, Demo. Shared by every board.

Everything in "panes, by board" is the one thing a board supplies. Everything else is the shell.

## What this changes elsewhere

Its material on Mohr–Mascheroni and Poncelet–Steiner is good and should survive somewhere as a story idea (the awkwardness of circles alone is a story, not a door). Proposed: mark the Spine as history, and let this page carry the order.
