# Theophilus's World

Michael, 25 Sept: put Theophilus at the middle of the line and show the world his materials came from. He is worth coming back to, because he is the one person who wrote down how the things in a workshop were actually made, which makes him a hub for materials rather than a one-story figure.

**The span.** From the trade that brought the colours, out to the rose windows and tracing floors that his method became.

## His events, above the line

- **about 1100** — a metalworker in what is now Germany, probably called Roger, at Helmarshausen. *The identification with Theophilus is the standard scholarly guess, not a fact.*
- **about 1120** — *On Divers Arts*. The whitewashed board, the recipes for coloured glass, the casting of lead.

## The world's events, below the line

Each is followed by its `why` for this story — one sentence, in the story's voice.

- **1079 — Omar Khayyam's calendar, in Persia.** Working at the observatory at Isfahan for Malik-Shah, he measured the year as 365.24219858 days. The true figure today is 365.24219. *Why:* while a workshop in Germany was writing down how to make blue glass, a man in Persia had measured the length of the year to eight decimal places, and Europe would go on using a worse calendar for another five hundred years.
- **1086 — Domesday Book, England.** *Why:* a whole country written down, in the same generation that a monk wrote down a whole craft.
- **1088 — Shen Kuo describes the magnetised needle, in China.** *Dream Pool Essays*: the first detailed account of the compass needle, including that it does not point quite true north. He does not mention using it at sea.
- **1119 — Zhu Yu's *Pingzhou Table Talks*, in China.** Written between about 1111 and 1117, published 1119: the first book anywhere to say that sailors steer by the needle. "In dark weather they look at the south-pointing needle." *Why:* the same few years that produced Theophilus's book of how-to produced another one on the other side of the world, and this one tells you how to find your way in the dark.
- **about 1115 — Abelard teaching in Paris.** *Unchecked this pass.* *Why:* people walked for weeks to stand in a yard and hear an argument.
- **about 1122 — Angkor Wat begun**, under Suryavarman II (reigned 1113 to about 1150); finished about 1150. *Why:* the largest religious building anyone has ever put up was started the same decade as the book, and took about as long as a cathedral.
- **about 1100 — Cahokia near its height**, on the Mississippi. *Unchecked this pass beyond the general dating.* *Why:* the biggest city north of Mexico, built of earth, with no writing to tell us what its people called it.
- **Lapis lazuli from Sar-e-Sang, in Badakhshan, carried west.** Effectively the only source Europe knew, then and for centuries. **Shared with Vermeer.** *Why for this story:* the blue in a window is a metal stirred into the melt, but the blue in a painting was a stone, dug out of one valley in Afghanistan and carried four thousand miles — which is why, for a long time, blue paint cost more than gold. **Its `why` in Vermeer's story ends differently**, on the turban. Same record, two last lines.
- **about 1220 — the rose windows at Chartres.** *Unchecked this pass.* *Why:* a hundred years after the book, the same method, a hundred times the size.
- **about 1330s — cobalt carried from Persia to China, and blue-and-white porcelain begins.** Imported cobalt, ground and painted onto the body before glazing; the earliest firmly dated pieces are the temple vases of 1351. **Shared with the palettes.** *Why:* the same metal that made Theophilus's blue turned up two hundred years later at the other end of Asia, and made the most copied pottery in the world.

## The two errors in the 25 Sept proposal

Written down so nobody puts them back.

**Cobalt and Chinese porcelain were in the wrong century.** The proposal had Persian cobalt colouring Chinese porcelain as a contemporary of Theophilus. It isn't: imported cobalt and blue-and-white belong to the second quarter of the *fourteenth* century, with the earliest dated pieces in 1351 — roughly two hundred years after the book. Song porcelain in Theophilus's own time was not blue-and-white at all. The event stays, but as a later one, and the `why` becomes the better sentence: the same metal, two centuries on and six thousand miles away.

**The compass event was attributed to the wrong book.** Shen Kuo in 1088 described the needle but said nothing about navigation. The first text anywhere to describe steering a ship by it is Zhu Yu's, written about 1111–1117 and published in 1119 — which is very nearly the same year as *On Divers Arts*. That is a better fact than the one it replaces: two how-to books, one decade, opposite ends of the world. Both events are worth keeping, in that order.

**One smaller fix.** Angkor Wat was begun about 1122, not 1113; 1113 is Suryavarman II's accession.

## Still unchecked

Abelard's dates in Paris, the Chartres rose windows, and the detail of Cahokia's peak. Each is plausible and each is stated here as approximate; none should ship without a source.
