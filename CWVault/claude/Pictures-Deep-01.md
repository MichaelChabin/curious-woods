# Pictures, batch 1: the twelve periods of the Mammals line, the Ice line and the last ice age

Read first, in the brief's order: 00-WHAT-CW-IS.md, Story-Voice.md twice, the Rulings' last three sections and its image rule (16 Sept: every image is evidence, not decoration; nothing she can already picture), the pictures insert, and Periods-Deep-03.md whole, each More against its wanted line. The ledger, `Pictures-Ledger.md`, did not exist and was made in this round from every *Pictures wanted* line in the seventeen batch files: 408 wanted pictures, 26 of them in this batch.

## Round 1: the sort

The rule applied to each candidate, before its bin: does the picture answer a question the More raises, and is it the real thing the More stands on. One picture per More; two where the More raises two questions.

**1. Recovery** (`recovery`). The More opens in the Cerrejón mine, on its leaves and *Titanoboa*. Two questions: are the leaves real and that big; was the snake real.
- A giant fossil leaf from Cerrejón: **findable, to try.** Scott Wing's leaves are Smithsonian specimens (STRI and the National Museum of Natural History); Smithsonian Open Access is CC0 if the specimen is imaged. Else *ask* (the Florida Museum).
- A *Titanoboa* vertebra beside an anaconda's: **ask** (the Florida Museum's photograph). Commons holds the Smithsonian's life-size model, a reconstruction; it would need a caption saying so, and the vertebra answers the question better. On the ask list.
- Proposed: the leaf.

**2. Heat** (`heat`). The More opens on Ellesmere Island, Strathcona Fiord, about 52 million years ago, and on dawn redwoods.
- The fossil forest stumps: **findable, to try, but a lookalike.** The stumps on Commons, if any, are Axel Heiberg Island's (about 45 million years, the next island west); the Geological Survey of Canada's photographs are Crown copyright, not free; the Carnegie Museum is *ask*. The More names only Ellesmere and makes no comparison with Axel Heiberg, so under the rule a photograph of Axel Heiberg is *none* unless Michael allows an honest caption ("on the next island, a few million years later"). Question 1.
- A living dawn redwood: **findable** (Commons, many; CC BY-SA likely). She cannot picture a dawn redwood, and the More's "a kind that still grows wild in China" asks what it looks like.
- Proposed: the dawn redwood; the stumps if Michael allows.

**3. Cooling** (`cooling`). The More is one wall of the Badlands, with *Mesohippus* in its layers.
- The striped walls: **findable** (National Park Service, public domain; sure).
- A *Mesohippus* skeleton: **findable** (Commons; several museum mounts). The caption must say which museum and whether it is a cast; the trap list's "a cast as the original".
- Proposed: both. The wall is the scene; "three toes on each foot" is a second question only a skeleton answers.

**4. Grass** (`grass`). The More is the Hubbard Rhino Barn at Ashfall, and *Deinotherium*'s tusks.
- The rhino skeletons in the barn: **findable** (Commons has Ashfall photographs; the Nebraska photographer Ammodramus releases his as public domain, to confirm per file).
- A *Deinotherium* skull: **findable** (Commons; the Vienna and other museum skulls).
- Proposed: both. The hole under the barn is the scene; "tusks that curved down from its lower jaw" is the second question.

**5. Ice** (`ice`). The More is Kap København, the bare ground where the forest was.
- Kap København today: **findable, to try.** Kjær and others 2022 (*Nature*) is open access, CC BY, and its first figure is believed to carry photographs of the site and the sediments; round 2 opens it. Else *ask* (the Natural History Museum of Denmark).
- Beth Zaiken's painting of the forest with mastodons: **ask** (the artist's; a reconstruction, allowed only captioned as a drawing and whose). On the ask list.
- Proposed: the site, from the paper.

**6. Lucy** (`lucy`, the period). The More is Hadar and Laetoli.
- Lucy's skeleton laid out: **findable** (Commons; photographs of casts in Cleveland, Washington and elsewhere, and some of the original on its 2007 to 2013 tour). The caption says cast or original, honestly. One file, shared with the event *Lucy* (question 5).
- The Laetoli footprints: **findable, as a cast.** The trail itself is reburied; the photographs that exist are of casts (the Smithsonian's) or of the site before reburial. The caption says cast.
- Proposed: both; two places, two questions.

**7. Hand axes** (`hand-axes`). The More is Kokiselei's hand axes and the Nariokotome boy.
- A Kokiselei hand axe: **ask.** The photographs are Lepre and others 2011 (*Nature*, not open) and the West Turkana Archaeological Project's. The More says "Hand axes like these were made for more than a million and a half years, across Africa, Europe and Asia", which is the More's own comparison; if Michael allows, a free photograph of another hand axe (the Smithsonian's, CC0) captioned "a hand axe from elsewhere, the same shape" would stand in. Question 2.
- The Nariokotome skeleton: **findable, as a cast** (Commons; the original is in Nairobi).
- Proposed: the hand axe if allowed, and the boy.

**8. Mammoths** (`mammoths`). The More is three teeth from the Siberian permafrost.
- A mammoth tooth from the permafrost: **findable, to try.** Van der Valk and others 2021 (*Nature*) is believed open access, CC BY; whether its figures show the molars is for round 2. Else *ask* (the Swedish Museum of Natural History, Love Dalén).
- A woolly mammoth skeleton: **none, proposed.** She can picture a mammoth, and a skeleton shows neither the hair nor the small ears the More describes. Question 4.
- Proposed: the tooth.

**9. The last ice age** (`the-last-ice-age`). The More is the NEEM camp and the folded ice at its bottom.
- The NEEM camp: **ask** (the Niels Bohr Institute); Commons to try, since participants sometimes upload.
- A piece of NEEM ice with its bubbles: **ask.** A public-domain photograph of core ice from another core (the US National Science Foundation's ice core facility, NOAA) exists, but it is a lookalike, and the More does not compare; *none* unless Michael allows "what core ice looks like" as the caption. Question 3.
- Proposed: none for now; both on the ask list.

**10. Hippos in London** (`hippos-in-london`). The More is the gravel under Trafalgar Square and the Natural History Museum's hippo tooth.
- The hippo tooth from Trafalgar Square: **findable, to try.** The NHM Data Portal releases specimen images CC BY 4.0, if this canine is imaged; the museum's web photograph is theirs, *ask*.
- The Barrington hippo: **findable, to try** (a visitor's photograph of the Sedgwick Museum's mounted skeleton on Commons). The More does not name Barrington; it stands for "more than thirty-five places".
- Proposed: the tooth; the Barrington hippo as the second if found.

**11. Ice sheets** (`ice-sheets`). The More is Doggerland, the fishing boats and the *Colinda* point.
- A mammoth tooth hauled up by a fishing boat: **findable, to try** (Commons, the Natuurhistorisch Museum Rotterdam's North Sea fossils); else *ask*.
- The *Colinda* point: **ask.** The wanted line says the British Museum; the point is believed to be in Norwich Castle Museum, to confirm in round 2, and the museum asked.
- **Map**, not on the wanted line: CW's own map at about 20,000 years ago, the southern North Sea and the Bering plain as land. The ice-age sea is already on the join bench's map (Stage 5), so this is the one picture the house can make itself, and it is the one the More most wants ("The southern North Sea was a plain of rivers, hills and marshes"). Question 5.
- Proposed: the map, and the tooth if found.

**12. After the ice** (`after-the-ice`). The More is Gobero's graves and the drawings of Tassili n'Ajjer.
- The rock paintings of Tassili n'Ajjer with hippos and cattle: **findable** (Commons, many; cattle sure, a hippo to find). The licence must be the photographer's own, never a "public domain" tag on a photograph of a rock wall, the trap list's second item.
- The Gobero site: **findable** (Sereno and others 2008, *PLOS ONE*, CC BY; its figures show the site and the burials, cited to the paper).
- Proposed: both; the graves are the scene, the drawings the second question.

**The event *Lucy*** (`lucy`, the event). Its wanted cast is the period's: one file. Hadar's gullies: **findable, to try** (Commons); it answers "a gully at Hadar", what the place looks like.

**Least sure of:** the three paper figures (Kjær 2022, van der Valk 2021, Sereno 2008), which rest on each paper being CC BY and its figure being a photograph rather than a map; the NHM Data Portal having imaged the Trafalgar Square canine; and whether any Axel Heiberg or NEEM photograph on Commons is the uploader's own.
