## The shallow seas are blue

What Michael sees now, at about 90 Ma: the seaway across North America is grey, and the continental shelf off the coast is blue. Two kinds of shallow sea, two colours.

That came from a mistake in step 12's prompt, and it is Michael's. The grey belongs to a later picture, the Earth with its oceans drained. There, rock that is under water today will be grey: dark grey to black where the sea was deep, lighter grey where it was shallow. Step 12 put that grey under the water too early, and only in some places.

What he wants now, with the water in:

- Every shallow sea reads as blue. The seaways that flood a continent and the shelves off its coasts are the same thing, and take the same colour.
- It is a lighter blue than the deep ocean, so she can tell shallow from deep at a glance, and plainly not land.
- No seam where a seaway meets a shelf.
- No grey on the Earth while the oceans are full.
- Dry land keeps its picture. The deep ocean keeps its blue.
- The soft shorelines from step 12 stay. The tears stay straight and sharp.
- Hide shallow seas and `?seas=0` still work, and hide both kinds.

Keep what you built for the grey, switched off, for the day the oceans are drained. Say where it lives and what turns it on. The bench switch for the crust may go, or stay with blue as the way the page opens; say which you chose. (The chat's default: it goes.)

Say what the two blues are, and why the shelf and the seaway differed before: were they drawn from two sets of data, or one set drawn two ways?

Pictures at 480 Ma, 201 Ma, 90 Ma and 69 Ma.

## A selected event's place turns to face her

Step 12 marks the place of a selected event on the globe. From now on the globe also turns so that place is in the middle.

- When an event that has a place is selected, the globe eases round until the place faces her. It arrives upright. No zoom.
- This holds in Time and in The World, and on the flat map as far as centring means anything there. Say what the map does.
- An event with no place moves nothing.
- If the handle also has to travel to the event's year, the turn and the travel happen together, as they do on arrival.
- Under reduced motion, a cut.
- If she is dragging the globe when it starts, her finger wins. (The chat's default.)
- The globe does not turn for events the handle merely passes, in a drag or later in play. Only a selection turns it. (The chat's default.)
- Her own markers, from Place markers, never turn the globe.
