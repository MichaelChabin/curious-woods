**3. The piece she means.** No round cursor on this board. With a colour chosen, the piece of glass under the tip of the mouse wears a thin copper outline as the mouse moves, before she presses (`mac-08`); a press — by finger or by mouse — outlines the piece under the tip, a slide moves the outline to whatever piece is under it now, and the release colours that piece, however far the hand went and nothing panning meanwhile (`mac-10`: a finger pressed on the lens and slid to the left crescent; `mac-11`: the crescent coloured on release; `ipad-03`, `ipad-04` the same by a real finger). The piece is found at most once a frame, only when the tip, the record, the view or the free frame has moved, and nothing of it enters the record (THE PIECE SHE MEANS in `glass.js`: `tipPieceNow`, `drawTipPiece`, `tipPressStart`, the state `PIECE_PRESSED`). With no colour chosen a tap colours as before and a drag on glass still pans.

**The leak — found and fixed.** The cause is in the engine's crossing registration, not in the table's arrangement. When a new circle or line crosses an old one within the merge radius (2.5 % of a unit) of a point already there, the crossing was dropped — correct where the old point lies on both curves (three circles through one point), wrong where it lies on only one of them: a *near miss*, under the merge radius but over the on-curve tolerance (0.25 % of a unit for a circle, 0.05 % for a line). The other curve then went undivided there, so the face walk ran straight along it across the first curve, and one tap coloured across several lead lines. The pairs of points almost on top of each other in Michael's screenshot are the signature: two of three nearly concurrent crossings registered, the third dropped. The fix (`regX`, `joinCurves`): a crossing that lands on a point already there now puts that point on the two curves that cross there where it is not on them already, so both are divided at it. Shown with a three-step construction found by a search for exactly this case (`leak-near-miss.json`: a line from 0 to the bottom-right corner, a circle from the top-right corner through 1, a circle from 0 through the bottom-left corner; the two circles cross 0.025 m from where the line crosses the second): the same four taps round the near miss on the committed tree and on this one — on the committed tree the first tap's yellow runs across the big circle's arc into the next piece (`mac-20b`), on this tree it stops at the arc and each piece takes its own colour (`mac-20`). This is an engine change, on every board; the harness says the three other boards are identical in every state (below), since none of their states has a near miss.

## What needs Michael's eye

1. **The sunlight range.** Dim is 60 % of the colour at the far left, bright is halfway to white at the far right (`SUN_DIM`, `SUN_BRIGHT`); the middle is exact. `mac-13` and `mac-14` show the ends. Dark glass dims to near black; pale glass brightens to near white.
2. **The outline's look.** A 1.5 px copper line round the piece, the point colour, at 0.9 (`mac-08`, `mac-10`, `ipad-03`). It follows the mouse whenever a colour is chosen, box open or closed; after New, clear glass counts as the colour chosen, so the outline follows the mouse from the first piece.
3. **The column with lines passing behind it.** Parchment through, a line at its left edge as at its right (`mac-07b`, `ipad-02`). The rules above and below the board run the full width in place of the old box; the sides are gone.
4. **The boxes at the column's foot.** All three open there, not only the Color box; in the pane at 1440 the box overhangs the column by 30 px each side, and at 1028 or on the iPad it stands against the left edge. A box she has dragged reopens where she left it, as before.
5. **Save with a name.** The quiet overwrite of 7 Oct is gone on this board: Save always asks, the same name overwrites, a new name is a new file.
6. **The table on the iPad upright** is 372 × 248 pt in an 826 pt board: whole and clear of the column, as the prompt's geometry gives, but small.
