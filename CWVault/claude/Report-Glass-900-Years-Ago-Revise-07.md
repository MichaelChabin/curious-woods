2. **The twelve-petal rose's overlaps** are amber, like the second rose, as the prompt's first guess. The alternative is red (like the first rose) or a third colour; the script colours by a one-line rule and can be changed in a minute.
3. **The Gothic window does not come out as the look page draws it** (`mac-14-gothic-window-the-glass` beside `look-05-gothic`). The round window "just touches every arch around it", and that is the trouble: at three places a construction circle crosses an arch within the engine's tolerances of where the round window touches it — the circle from step 10 crosses the first circle 0.028 units from the touch, and lands 0.0012 units from the round window; the circle from step 9 the same on the right; and near the big arch two crossings fall 0.016 units apart. The engine joins such a point to the round window (its on-circle tolerance is 0.0025 units, its merge radius 0.025), so the round window and the arch share two vertices a few pixels apart with near-identical arcs between them, and the face walk, ordering edges at those vertices, runs two real pieces together through the pinch: the round window's lower-left quarter with the first arch's right-hand piece, its lower-right with the second arch's left-hand piece, and its upper half with the top of the big arch. The script colours such a piece by the majority of its samples (blue, blue, red), so what shows is honest to what a tap there would do: the lower quarters of the round window come out blue and the top of the big arch red, with slivers of purple where the walk did find a piece. Nothing was changed in the engine for this — it is the tolerance model meeting an exactly tangent design, on every board alike, and not a thing to patch inside this revision. Two ways out, neither mine to choose: a design whose round window stops short of touching (a hair smaller, or centred a hair higher, so the touches become clear crossings or clear gaps), or a tolerance change in the engine's face walk, which the harness would then have to hold to on every board. Until one is chosen the demo plays as it is and the mismatch is visible.
4. **A demo's place on the board.** A demo opens centred in the area right of the column, at the home scale, so a small design (the quatrefoil, the six-petal rose) sits in the middle of that area rather than on 0 and 1's home places; *Reset view* then shows, and takes her home. The Gothic window, six units wide, fits at the home scale centred on 1.
5. **The glass as one step.** The construction grows a circle or a line at a time and the whole window is glazed at once; with 48 pieces in the quatrefoil, a piece a step would have been forty-eight taps. If he would rather watch the glass go in piece by piece, it is one flag.

## 6 Nov: the overdraw in the Gothic window — the cause found

Michael's picture of the demo on the site (6 Nov, "extra areas were colored. This might be a selection issue"): red above the round window up to the circle of radius 2 about 1, outside the big arch altogether, and the round window's lower quarters blue. Replayed in Node through `tools/glass-log.js`: the cause is in the engine's crossing registration, not in the demo and not in the design.

- **Where it goes wrong.** The circle of radius 1 1/2 about 0 (step 9) crosses the big arch's left side 0.015 units from where it crosses the round window. The merge radius is 0.025, so the second crossing is absorbed into the first point, and revision 5's leak fix (`regX`, `joinCurves`) then declares that point on the round window — 0.015 off it, six times the on-curve tolerance. The walk has a vertex on the round window that is not on it, and from there it runs out through the top of the window into the region above the arch. Lower down, the same circle crosses the small arch 0.028 from the touch and lands 0.0012 off the round window, within the on-curve tolerance (0.0025), so the engine puts that point on the round window too: two circles that only touch are given a second shared vertex, which the walk reads as a crossing, and the lower quarters run into the small arches.
- **The rule that fixes it, tested in Node on a scratch copy of `glass.js`.** A crossing that lands within the merge radius of a point already there is that point only when the point lies on both curves within floating error (one millionth of a unit); otherwise it is a crossing of its own, a hair from the other point. And a point joins a curve it was not constructed on only within that same floating error, not within a quarter of a percent. With that: the Gothic window cuts as the look page draws it, 32 pieces, none fused (`overdraw-02-…png` beside `overdraw-01-…png`, the demo as committed); the other five demos come out identical piece by piece; the revision-5 near miss (`leak-near-miss.json`'s geometry) still colours piece by piece, with two slivers of its own between the points that were merged before. The price is honest: where a construction nearly concurs, there are now two or three points a hair apart (as there already are in revision 5's screenshot) and the slivers between them are pieces.
- **Landed, 6 Nov, on Michael's word, "Yes, land it".** In `glass.js`: `regX` merges a crossing into a point already there only when `liesOn` says the point lies on both curves within `_onTol` (a millionth of a unit, set with the merge radius at init); `_integrateNewPoint` and a new line's or circle's gathering of existing points at replay use the same `_onTol` in place of the default quarter of a percent; the comment THE OVERDRAW above `regX` says why. The Gothic demo rebuilt by `tools/glass-demos-theophilus.js`: 32 pieces, none fused; the other five rewritten identical byte for byte. The harness in this pane, the committed tree (`9d5023c2`, a worktree) against this one: the circles board, the lab and the Wright window identical in every state (`results/r07b-*.json`).
