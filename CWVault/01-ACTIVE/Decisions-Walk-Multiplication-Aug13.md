## 5. Mode Explore — Michael's sketch (dictated, not yet walked)

The first and simplest mode. Five tools:

**Multiply** (demoted from mode to tool; the introduction). Tap a pane on one
axis, then a pane on the other; rectangles appear and all the products are
created in place with prime factors showing. Tapping an empty axis pane
instantiates that number and draws a rectangle one unit wide reaching the
length of the other axis — the cue that the next tap belongs there. The second
tap instantiates its number if the pane is empty, then completes the
multiplication.

**Choose / Select** (was "Explore"). Reachable at any time; never clears the
plane. Tapping an empty pane instantiates that number and the numbers and
rectangles that produced it, including along the axes. Click-and-drag starting
on an empty pane creates a rectangle and instantiates any number below it;
starting on a filled pane creates a rectangle and deletes any number below it;
a drag within one row or column affects only that row or column.

**Rectangles only.** Numbers shown as rectangles with the value in the upper
right corner; rectangles emerge from both sides; reached by selecting a pane or
dragging from a number on either axis. Controls: each new rectangle gets a
different outline colour; keep earlier rectangles (draw new over old); a
two-rectangle comparison (see open question 1 — the dictated words mixed
"difference" and "union"). Option to keep or replace previous rectangles.

**Restack.** Draw a rectangle, tap a number on the x axis: the rectangle's
panes ungroup and animate into stacks of that width. A remainder is sliced into
divisor-many equal slices and distributed — 4 restacked by 3 gives three
columns plus the fourth pane in thirds, 1⅓ per stack (pending confirmation,
open question 2). Division as a physical act; fractions arrive with the
remainder.

**Make Square.** Draw a rectangle, tap Make square: the panes separate and
arrange into the largest square; the remainder is subdivided decimally (dictated
as ten rows and ten columns — see open question 3); readout: root n ≈ n.n, to
tenths.

## 6. The decimals argument — why Restack and Make Square are tempting

Michael's framing: nothing about division or square roots fails to make sense
to a child presented this way; the catch is they implicitly introduce decimals.
The case that this is an arrival, not an intrusion:

- The plane already speaks decimals. The lattice ladder crosses decades and the
  map-reveal bench's deep zoom constructs the tenths. Decimals are the lattice
  one decade down; a child who has zoomed has stood on them.
- The two remainders teach the two notations honestly. Restack slices by the
  divisor; Make Square slices by ten, always. Side by side, a child can notice
  that ten is a choice, not a law — decimals are fractions where someone
  pre-committed to ten. The contrast says it without a word of explanation.
- The act precedes the notation. She watches the slicing; the readout records
  what she saw. Decimals arrive as measurement — Intuition #11, measure by
  counting, refine to converge.
- The rule that follows: **the readout never speaks more precision than the act
  showed.**
- The lurking gift: Make Square at 2. The remainder never resolves — tenths
  give 1.4, finer gives 1.41, it never closes. The door to irrationality,
  unannounced, same shape as the beat-tuner's near-miss.
